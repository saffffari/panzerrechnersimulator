# Soviet 45 mm anti-tank guns — 28 September 2026

The owner asked whether the Soviets had anti-tank guns at this stage, adding that "infantry being
able to man and fire an anti-tank gun could increase their danger". They did: the 45 mm model 1937
(53-K) was the standard anti-tank gun at every level, and from December 1942 each rifle battalion
had a platoon of two. Sources: `docs/research/SOVIET_AT_WEAPONS_BRIEF_20260928.md`.

## What the game does (`src/at_guns.rs`)

- **Two guns** in the winter encounter, the rifle battalion's anti-tank platoon, sited for
  flanking fire on the route north out of the courtyard, the way the Tiger must go to reach the
  tank platoons. The sites were chosen on the real terrain for their field of fire (a probe scored
  candidates by how much of that route they can see: the west site sees all of it, the east one
  most) and lie beyond point-blank of the spawn and the house. Platoon A's beat moved 60 m north
  to stay clear of the east gun.
- **Crews of four** at each gun (of the six-man detachment), drawn from the garrison soldiers,
  who otherwise stand near the world origin far from this map. They are ordinary soldiers: they
  can be shot, and high explosive kills them as it does any infantry.
- **Doctrine** (documented: flanking fire from concealment, fire opened suddenly at close range,
  scarce tungsten for close flank shots, going to ground when overrun):
  - hidden and silent until the fight has started — a gunshot the crew hears (the Tiger's gun,
    its own platoon's other gun) or the tank platoons engaged — and the Tiger is in its sector
    within 400 m; at 200 m it fires whatever else is happening;
  - it sees only as far as the blizzard allows (the tank crews' vehicle envelope), and only with
    a clear line of sight;
  - it traverses ±30° on its carriage; beyond that the crew hauls the trails round (10 s);
  - aimed fire about 7 rounds a minute (8.5 s), slower short-handed;
  - flank aspect: the lower side over the fuel tanks, where the road wheels usually take it; front:
    sprocket, driver's visor, sprocket, ball mount in turn; rear: rear plate and idlers; it leads
    a moving Tiger by the flight time;
  - two tungsten-core rounds per gun, only for flank or rear shots within 350 m;
  - the crew goes to ground if the Tiger closes inside 100 m and re-mans once it is past 150 m.
- **Rounds**: BR-240 armour-piercing (1.43 kg, 760 m/s) and BR-240P tungsten-core (0.85 kg,
  985 m/s) as authoritative projectiles through the same flight, armour and exterior-damage path
  as the T-34s' shells, with the gun report heard after its sound delay.
- **Riflemen man the gun** (the owner's idea, and the documented emergency practice): when a gun's
  artillerymen are all dead and the gun still stands, the nearest living riflemen within 60 m run
  to it and serve it, worse (1.8 × dispersion, slower service).
- **Destroying a gun**: any cannon round through its shield and carriage wrecks it; high explosive
  bursting within a few metres wrecks it; MG fire stops on the shield (the crew can still be
  shot). A wrecked gun's upper carriage lies knocked off its pivot.

## In the streets (urban-intro district, 29 September 2026)

On the urban-intro district the courtyard's two sites were screened by the new buildings: gun A
fired no shot in the ambush test, and gun B's site stood in a lane. The district tool had proposed
street sites from building footprints and terrain alone. The encounter now reads its layout from the
manifest revision: a revision with a `street_encounter` block in its edits (written by
`tools/worldgen/urban_intro_district.py`, parsed strictly by `src/street_plan.rs`) is laid in the
streets; the courtyard workshop has none and keeps the sites above exactly (its ambush test replays
shot for shot as before).

- **Candidates, not sites, from the district.** The tool offers 1,940 places a gun could be dug in:
  the rubble of the 23 razed plots on a 4 m grid, and three points outside every corner of every
  standing building, outside every hard box, off the tram street's carriageway, at least 150 m from
  the spawn and 12 m off the platoons' beats. Documented practice: anti-tank fire in the town came
  "mainly from ambushes, using breaches in fences and walls, doorways, gates, windows" (Chuikov,
  S3 p. 298), with flanking fire from neighbouring buildings covering the approaches (S3 p. 296).
- **The kill zone** is the tram street north out of the courtyard, from the house's latitude to
  60 m beyond the district's edge (the way the Tiger must go to reach the tanks), weight 1, and the
  Tiger's other ways out north: streets A and B between the side streets W1 and E1 and the side
  streets' exits north of street B, weight 0.5 (the lanes 0.25).
- **The field-of-fire probe runs on the built district** (`src/street_encounter.rs`) when the
  encounter starts. A candidate needs ground for the gun and its crew (nothing within 2 m, under
  0.8 m of relief), 25 m of walkable ground behind it for the crew and riflemen to come up, and it
  must not see the spawn or the house within its 400 m opening range. Its field is every kill-zone
  point (8 m apart) between 100 m (inside that the crew goes to ground) and 400 m that the gun
  commander's eye sees through the game's own sight test and that its round reaches (no standing
  record, story cover, door, tree or terrain between muzzle and the Tiger's lower side), inside
  the sector it will be laid on or within point blank. A point scores its line's weight times
  0.25 + 0.75 × the sine between the line of fire and the Tiger's travel (flanking fire counts four
  times fire along the street) times a range weight (1 from 120 to 350 m). The platoon's two guns
  are the best pair at least 60 m apart that do not fire into each other.
- **Where it puts them.** On the generated district, 443 of the 1,940 candidates can fight
  (898 see no kill zone, 299 have no ground, 242 no approach from behind, 46 see the spawn or the
  house, 12 stand on ground too steep). Gun A stands in the rubble of the razed apartment block
  `c1r0.w00` at the corner of lane W2 and lane A (1626, -86), laid east down lane A (96°): it sees
  the lane A crossing and 72 m of the tram street's exit into the open ground, the side streets'
  exits and lane A itself, 59 points at 104-352 m, mean flank sine 0.53. Gun B stands beside the
  corner of building `c1r0.w04` on street B (1630, 42.5), laid east down street B (91°): it sees
  the street B crossing of the tram street broadside and street B itself, 37 points at 101-324 m.
  Both stand west of the tram street. The best site east of it, the rubble of `c3r0.e01` by the E1
  crossing of lane A (29 points, mean flank sine 0.81), would face gun A straight down lane A, so
  the pair rule refuses it; the receipt records the best three sites on each side. The probe takes
  0.6-0.7 s at the encounter's start (2.6 s once under load).
- The staging for tests and films (`street_at_gun_stage`) puts the Tiger on a point of the gun's
  own field, the tram street first, where its hull stands clear and its eye sees the whole gun
  position.

## Limits and follow-ups

- The gun is a low-poly mesh built from its published dimensions; its hit box is the shield and
  carriage (barrel and trails are not targets). Winter whitewash on the gun is assumed.
- No relocation by hand to alternate positions; no dug-in pit.
- In the streets: no gun stands inside a building (the documented embrasures in windows, breaches
  and half-basements need a gun inside a ruin's ground floor and a sight test through its opening);
  both guns cover the northern crossings from the west; the probe's weights (flank 0.25 + 0.75 sine,
  the range band, the kill zone's line weights) are tuning, not measured doctrine.
- The Tiger's armour table has 80 mm for the whole hull side; the historical 60 mm lower side
  behind the wheels, and the wheels as spaced armour, are not modelled yet.
- Anti-tank rifle teams (14.5 mm PTRD, the commonest infantry anti-tank weapon), grenade and bottle
  tank-hunters, the ZiS-3 and the 85 mm AA gun in direct fire are not yet in the encounter.
