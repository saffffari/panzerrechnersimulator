# The sky, the night and the eye — 2 November 2026

The owner's direction (2 November 2026, evening): "work on the sky and environment lighting and
day night cycle and sunrise/sunset transition effects. i also want to add more stars, make the
stars brighter, but keep stars to a single in game pixel size. moon and sun discs can also be a
bit bigger. and moon should have phases with 10 days moon phase cycle. also tune the night
lighting so that we aren't at this pitch black look. there should be a bit of light even at night
and it shouldn't be black but a deep blue tone. we shouldn't change the lighting look inside the
tank: some versions i've seen change the colour inside and the interior lights, which isn't
correct."

## What is already there

The sky is not a palette. `src/ephemeris.rs` places the Sun and the Moon by Meeus for the act's
date, place and running clock (`celestial_sky::SkyClock`); `src/physical_environment.rs` scatters
their light through a spectral atmosphere (Rayleigh, Mie, ozone, a cloud layer, the airglow
layer at 90 km with the La Palma moonless-sky radiance) and shows their discs at their real angular
size; the raymarch backend draws each star of a seeded lattice into exactly one internal pixel
(`shaders/rm_shade.wgsl` `rm_pixel_star`, 2026-10-28). The eye (`src/observer.rs`,
`shaders/exposure.wgsl`) meters the frame, adapts pupil, pigments and neural gain, and maps the
scene to the display by Ferwerda's threshold matching with a cool, luminance-neutral rod hue
(`ROD_DISPLAY_HUE`) and an outdoor rod display gain of 6. The day cycle runs the clock faster
(`KESSEL_DAY_CYCLE_SCALE`, 6 in the demo launcher: a ninety-minute day); the bodies still stand
where the ephemeris puts them. `docs/research/LIGHT_NIGHT_ADAPTATION_20260929.md` is the sourced
basis; `docs/PHYSICAL_TRUTH_AUDIT.md` "Light, sky and the eye" the ledger.

So the direction is not "add a sky": it is to find why the night reads black and the stars dim
on the owner's screen, to compress the Moon's month as the day is compressed, to enlarge the
discs as a named presentation choice, to make the star field as populous as the real one, and to
prove the cabin unchanged.

## Measured first: `sky-review`

`cargo run --release -- sky-review <prefix>` (src/sky_review.rs) renders, through the shipping
path with the eye converged per still, the exterior over the act-1 Tiger at the solstice through
nautical and civil twilight, sunrise, the Dawn phase, noon, the Dusk phase, sunset and the evening
twilights, the Christmas Eve Moon (96 %, 59 deg up) and the moonless 6 January night, the stars
50 deg up on both nights, the moonlit night under six tenths cloud and under overcast, and the
loader's lamp-lit cabin at noon and at night. Each still carries the scene photometry, the eye's
state and the displayed picture's mean, blue-over-red ratio and black share in `<prefix>_sky.json`.
The baseline before any change is `captures/demo-20261101-sky_baseline_20261102a_sky_*`.

## The plan

1. **The Moon's month, compressed.** `SkyClock` carries a lunar cycle length (10 days by the
   owner's direction, `KESSEL_MOON_CYCLE_DAYS`; 29.530589 is the real synodic month and restores
   the ephemeris exactly). The Moon's geometric position is evaluated at the clock's start plus the
   elapsed time scaled by the ratio, so its phase, its rising and setting and its illuminance stay
   consistent with each other and with the Sun; only the month is shorter, as the day already is.
   A named approximation of the same kind as the day cycle, recorded in the audit.
2. **The discs.** The Sun's and the Moon's angular radii are scaled by `KESSEL_DISC_SCALE`
   (1.6 by default; 1 is the real sky). The irradiance is untouched, so the illumination and the
   disc's total light are unchanged and the disc's radiance falls by the square of the scale; the
   penumbrae widen with it. A presentation choice, named and gated.
3. **The stars.** The lattice keeps its single-pixel drawing and gains the real sky's population:
   about 9,000 stars to magnitude 6.5 with the real magnitude counts (N(<m) rising about
   tenfold per two magnitudes), each star's irradiance from its magnitude (V = 0 is 3.6e-11
   W m^-2 nm^-1 at 550 nm), its colour from blue-white to warm. The CPU reference, the sky shader
   and the raymarch pass share one law. Brightness on the display follows from the eye.
4. **The night.** Whatever the baseline shows: the moonless clear night field is about 2e-4
   cd/m^2 and a fully dark-adapted eye sees it; in play the eye reaches rod threshold only after
   thirty to forty minutes of real time while the night lasts thirty minutes of play, so the player
   lives in the cone plateau. The remedy that keeps every light as it is: the eye's slow pigment
   processes run at the sky clock's rate (the day is compressed six times; so is the crewman's
   dark adaptation), a reproduction choice named in the audit. The rod display hue is deepened
   toward blue outdoors only, luminance-neutral as before; the cabin keeps the hue it had.
5. **Sunrise and sunset.** The transport already does the physics; the baseline decides whether
   the transitions want more aerosol (the burning city's smoke is real and would redden them) or
   are right as they are.
6. **The interior.** The cabin lamps, the grade and the cabin's rod gain and hue are untouched;
   the interior stills before and after are compared by their photometry and their displayed
   statistics, and the numbers are in this file's status section.

## Status

**Baseline measured** (`captures/demo-20261101-sky_baseline_20261102b_sky_*`, the eye converged
per still): the moonlit Christmas Eve night displays at a mean of 0.114 (linear), 5 % black
pixels, blue over red 1.81; the moonless January night at 0.010, 14 % black; the stars overhead
on the moonless night 0.013, 1 % black, the lattice's single pixels plainly visible. The
lamp-lit interior at noon 0.040 and at the moonlit night 0.046 (blue over red 0.07 and 0.09: the
warm lamps). So the adapted night is blue-grey, not black; the black of live play is the eye
that never reaches its rods under the compressed day, which the pigment rate addresses.

**Landed** (this change): the Moon's ten-day month; the discs at 1.6x; the star population,
essentially white; the pigment rate; the deeper hue outdoors with the cabin's hue kept; and,
after the owner's "oversaturated, deep fried" sunset: the AgX tonemap in place of the ACES fit
(`KESSEL_TONEMAP=aces` keeps it), a winter continental aerosol (optical depth 0.08), ozone
averaged over the bands (the point samples painted civil twilight magenta), and a pastel
palette mode for the deltaP look (`KESSEL_LOOK=deltap-pastel`, green-blues first).

**After** (`captures/demo-20261101-sky_after_20261102a_sky_*`, AgX, aerosol, hue, stars; the same
converged eye): the moonless night displays at 0.013 (was 0.010), blue over red 3.5 (was 1.8);
the moonlit night 0.099 (was 0.114); the stars overhead white, about four times as many. The
lamp-lit interior: the scene photometry is identical to the last digit (hdr mean 1.674 cd/m^2 at
noon and 1.535 at night, before and after), the displayed mean 0.0404 to 0.0411 at noon and
0.0455 to 0.0459 at night (the tonemap's two per cent), blue over red 0.07 and 0.09 unchanged.
So no light in the cabin changed; `KESSEL_TONEMAP=aces` restores the former display of it exactly.

**Through the owner's look** (`captures/demo-20261101-sky_look_{aces,agx,pastel}_20261102a_sky_*`,
deltaP dithered colour at 1024 x 576): the ACES fit is the saturated orange sunset and purple
zenith; AgX is softer and pastel, still mauve through civil twilight (hence the ozone change,
`captures/demo-20261101-sky_ozone_20261102a_sky_*`); the pastel palette gives a painted sky in
teals and creams, banded in rings round a high Sun because the palette's sky ramp has few steps.
The owner's pick (2 November 2026, night, after seeing both): "agx is too realistic, we need the
pastel and dither": the pastel palette with the ordered dither (`deltap-pastel`) is the demo
launcher's default look; the dithered-colour mode stays an option. Open: a longer sky ramp in the palette (or a 64-entry palette at four times the pair search), the
owner's choice between the dithered-colour and the palette modes, and a hue control for the
continuous mode if the twilight's remaining mauve is still unwanted.
