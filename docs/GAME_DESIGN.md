# Kessel — GAME DESIGN (Core Concept)

The pitch, the loop, the win/lose, the feel. Subordinate to `DESIGN_PILLARS.md`. This is the canonical
statement of the game; expanded facets live in the sibling design docs under `docs/design/`.

## One-line pitch
A lone tank on the wrong side of a collapsing encirclement, retracing its own trail of ruin westward
through a ruined alternate-history campaign to reach what remains of its own side.

## The frame
The offensive that carried you east has failed. You are deep inside the pocket — the *Kessel* — alone
in a Tiger, the rest of your war dead or scattered. Friendly lines are to the **west**, the direction
you came from. That is the only thing you know. **Head west.** The same Tiger and crew then cross a
deliberately compressed alternate-history continuity from the fictional Stalingrad breakout to
Oderbruch and Halbe in 1945. This is not presented as documentary chronology.

## Structure
- **Authored open-world maps** assembled from deterministic procedural layers and then deliberately
  composed. Generation supplies density and variation; routes, encounters, landmarks and emotional
  scenes remain authored rather than randomized at runtime.
- Each act starts near its **eastern edge** and drives west toward an act-specific objective. Act I
  seeks escape; Act II's rendezvous is empty; Act III reaches the final platoon/finale rather
  than a generic row of waiting friendlies.
- The campaign is one multi-hour journey of attrition. In the current build, Acts I and II retry
  from their entrance checkpoints and Act III is one-attempt. Full-campaign permadeath remains a
  planned **Ironman mode**, not a description of current checkpoint behavior.

## The loop
- **Slow-burn exploration + driving** through the moody 3D world is the baseline — reading terrain,
  threading ruins, scavenging, conserving, listening.
- **Contact is intermittent; threat is constant.** Combat is high-stakes, intense and often brief;
  between contacts the player listens, works, diagnoses and chooses routes under pressure. Tension
  and release remain weighted toward dread without making the map uniformly loud.

## Resources & progression
- No generic hunger/thirst bars. What you manage is **ammunition, fuel, components, medical state,
  batteries and time**. Period consumables may alter stress, alertness or capability, but never act
  as unexplained health potions.
- **Upgrades and ammo are scavenged along the way** — wrecks, depots, the dead. Your tank grows
  stronger or weaker by what you find and what you lose. **Attrition is the pressure.**

## Story — the world speaks first
- Narrative is led by **environmental storytelling and physical evidence**. Crew callouts, radio
  traffic and the physical message/Enigma station may carry terse operational information, but there
  is no omniscient narrator or exposition-heavy cutscene layer.
- **The spine changes by act:** Act I retraces the failed eastward advance through its own burnt
  columns and shattered positions; Act II reads the destruction of home and the empty rendezvous;
  Act III follows physical evidence of the collapsing breakout toward the finale. The wasteland is
  the story, but not every wreck was made by the player.

## Feel
Slow, cold, lonely, dread-soaked driving — then sudden, brutal, decisive violence — then quiet again.
Every shell counts. Every shot risks the whole run.
