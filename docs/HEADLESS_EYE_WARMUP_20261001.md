# Frozen-scene capture eye warmup

The v3 incoming/coax inside receipts reported `stable:true` after 13 eye observations with opening cone exposure approximately 4223.82, while the opening video darkened over its first few tenths of a second. Diagnosis found two harness mismatches: repeated warmup renders held `Renderer::time` fixed, making observer `dt=0` after initialization; and warmup used camera-centered source sky maps whereas recorded gameplay used the tank-centered shared-shadow scope. No post-warmup exposure reset was found.

The combat repair is capture-only. Still/movie warmup begins and ends the same tank-centered shared-shadow scope as `gameplay_native_frame`. The first eye observation initializes through the existing shader sentinel. Every subsequent warmup step writes only state[1] (four bytes at offset 4) to the preceding 1/60-second observer timestamp, renders the unchanged scene, then restores the last-observation timestamp to the original scene clock. At scene time zero the temporary observer clock is 1/60 s and the previous timestamp is zero, avoiding an erroneous reinitialization through a negative timestamp. Any pre-existing observer-clock override is restored. Exposure, pupil, pigment, neural and flash-energy state are never overwritten by this helper.

The shader, live eye algorithm, source light powers, material transport, simulation/physics, sky/weather clock and audio are unchanged. The general headless maximum remains 480 observations, the relative threshold 0.001 and the consecutive stability requirement 12. A maximum-length run can still report `stable:false`; receipts must be checked rather than treating such a result as accepted. Root's incoming v4 capture completed in 91.99 s, reported stable exposure after 400 observations, and its first image was visually accepted. This verifies actual warmup progression for that occupied Loader scene, not every lighting configuration.

## Driver-title cache

The title screenshot uses the actual driver-title renderer, not the separate legacy LAN turntable. The former driver path rendered 64 scene frames at an unchanged observer time and then cached its LDR image without checking eye convergence. Its live counterpart behaved the same way. It does not call the general headless warmup, so the combat repair alone did not fix it.

The title now advances a scoped observer clock by actual elapsed time between live title frames. A title screenshot advances the same observer in deterministic 1/60-second steps. The live path renders at most one scene frame per event-loop call, retains the 64-frame transport minimum, and caches only after finite positive exposure observations meet the same 12-observation/0.001-relative-delta gate with advancing observer time. Invalidating the backdrop resets the cache/convergence gate and wall-clock tracker, preserving eye adaptation history. The original scene-time stamp is restored after every observation so resumed gameplay does not inherit a future observer clock.

The unsettled live title performs one small synchronous exposure-buffer readback per rendered frame. It can block that callback for GPU completion, but does not run a multi-frame warmup inside one input callback. The settled title uses the cached scene/UI path. Weather, physics, source powers and observer response equations remain unchanged.

The v5 menu run failed the unchanged gate at 480 observations (8 modeled seconds). An explicit diagnostic continuation held scene time 0.0083333338 s, camera and metered photopic/scotopic luminance fixed. Exposure decreased smoothly from 3562.515 to 241.805 and passed 12 consecutive relative changes below 0.001 at frame 976 (16.27 modeled seconds). The clock was progressing correctly. The static source aperture repeatedly enters the existing bright-flash response: flash energy and afterimage increased while the steady meter stayed fixed. This is measured convergence under the existing per-observation criterion, not proof that every observer state has reached mathematical equilibrium. Full sampled eye state is retained in `artifacts/runtime/title_eye_diagnostic_v6_20261001.json`; the failed v5 artifacts remain unchanged.

The ordinary headless title budget is now 1800 observations (30 modeled seconds), with margin over the measured 976. General headless captures retain 480; live behavior and the stability gate are unchanged. `KESSEL_TITLE_EYE_DIAGNOSTIC_FRAMES` can extend only this title budget to at most 3600 observations and enables eye-state logging. A changing or invalid eye still fails at the limit. Root inspected and accepted the diagnostic title PNG for this narrow clock repair: clean UI over the actual dark driver view and bright physical aperture.
