# The Tiger's layered damage model ("FEA damage") — design, 2 November 2026

Branch `claude/fea-damage-20261102`, cut from `f747353`. This is the stage-0 design written before
code. Later sections of this file record what each stage actually landed; where they disagree with
this design, the stage records and the code win. `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/PHYSICAL_TRUTH_AUDIT.md`
govern. The previous plan for this direction is `docs/ARMOR_FRAGMENT_DEVELOPMENT_20260929.md`; its
work bounds, its exhaustion rule and its list of tests to preserve are the baseline here.

## What the owner asked for

Shell damage where armour is penetrated physically, each impact deforms the plate uniquely (torn,
bent, punched), penetration is layered rather than yes/no (through the front lower plate and into
the transmission but not through its casing), non-exploding shells lodge and can be pulled out,
spall and shrapnel are physical objects that can be grabbed and thrown out, heat and air resistance
are simulated with glowing metal mapped to local temperature, air pressure is part of the model,
and slow-motion cross sections show which systems each kind of impact knocks out.

## Names, so nothing is over-claimed

- **"FEA damage"** is the owner's name for the whole model and the module (`src/fea_damage*`).
  The plate solver in it is **not a finite-element method**. It is a *voxel lattice*: one particle
  per cubic cell of the struck plate, bonded to its 18 nearest lattice neighbours, a bond-based
  peridynamic discretisation (Silling 2000) with per-bond elastic–plastic response and bond
  failure. It is named that way in code, docs and the ledger.
- **The outcome authority stays the calibrated ballistic law.** Whether a round perforates a plate,
  and its speed behind it, come from `ballistics.rs` (DeMarre with the project's slope and
  overmatch rules, Lambert–Jonas residual speed), gated by the existing calibration tests. The
  lattice is *driven* by that outcome: the projectile's motion through the plate is prescribed from
  the calibrated speed loss, and the lattice computes how the plate yields, tears, bends, plugs and
  heats around that motion. The lattice never decides a perforation on its own, so the published
  tables cannot drift. The work the lattice absorbs is compared with the kinetic energy the
  calibrated law removes, and the ratio is reported, not hidden.
- **Layered chain:** the shot line is followed past the struck plate through air, component
  casings, component interiors and the far wall, layer by layer, each with its own law (below).
- **No new human injury criteria.** Crew wounding stays in the existing crew model
  (`crew_damage`, `first_aid`, `living_crew_wounds`) with its existing thresholds. This work changes
  only what reaches it: real fragment and penetrator masses and speeds. Replacing the 0.24 J
  placeholder with a sourced criterion is not part of this branch.

## Geometry: frames, scale and the component envelopes

**Frames.** Hull-local metres, origin at ground contact, +X the vehicle's left, +Y up, +Z forward.
Turret parts are turret-local about `TIGER_PARTS.turret_pivot`; the gun cradle and breech rotate
about the trunnion with the gun.

**Measured scale finding (inherited, not fixed here).** The game's baked Tiger GLBs are
tessellations of the owner's kit CAD (Rye Field RM-5010, 1/35) at 0.0389198 m per kit mm, which is
1.112 × the kit's 1:35 (`docs/research/TIGER_CAD_COMPONENTS_20261102.md`, 446 solid↔node pairs,
RMS 0.17 mm). The baked hull is 7.13 m long and 4.12 m wide; the real Tiger I's hull is 6.32 m and
3.71 m over the battle tracks, which the kit at 1:35 reproduces. The game Tiger is therefore about
11 % larger than the vehicle it represents. This branch does not rescale the vehicle (that is the
main line's decision: it changes every collision, camera and interior constant). The damage model
compensates where it matters to truth: a component's homogenised interior density is divided by
1.112 so that the mass a shell meets along a path through the oversized envelope equals the real
component's (areal-density preserving). Plate and casing thicknesses are real thicknesses.

**Component envelopes.** `catalog/tiger_cad_components.v1.json` (generated by
`tools/tiger_cad_register.py` from the CAD) gives 27 identified components with oriented boxes,
solid volumes and measured envelope errors. The boxes are loose (empty fraction 0.21–0.92), so the
chain uses a voxelisation of the actual CAD solids instead: `tools/tiger_cad_voxelize.py` turns the
tessellated component meshes into run-length occupancy grids at 20 mm (game scale) in
`catalog/tiger_cad_component_voxels.v1.json` (small, committed; the meshes stay in the gitignored
`assets/fea/tiger_cad/`). Error bound: a voxel boundary lies within half a cell diagonal (17.3 mm)
of the true surface; the tool also reports the voxel volume against the exact solid volume per
component. Where the voxel file is absent the boxes are used and the ledger says so. Surface
normals for casing obliquity come from the occupancy gradient over a 3×3×3 neighbourhood (a
staircase normal, error reported per test case).

**Casings and contents.** The kit CAD is solid plastic: its wall thickness is not the real
casing. Each component therefore carries an authored casing (material, thickness) and a
homogenised interior (material, effective density from the component's real mass over its real
volume), every value an estimate with its provenance string in `fea_damage::components`. The
engine is the Maybach HL 210 P45 of the Stalingrad-period Tigers (aluminium-alloy crankcase), the
fuel tanks hold the game's live fuel state, the ammunition bins hold whatever rounds the game's
live ammunition says are there (the existing `live_ammunition_in_frame` cartridges stay the
authority for initiation).

|---|---|---|---|

## Materials (`fea_damage::materials`)

Generic engineering values, each with a provenance string: density, Young's modulus, initial yield
stress, linear hardening modulus, logarithmic strain-rate coefficient (Johnson–Cook form, generic
for steels), thermal softening to the melting point, failure strain, specific heat, conductivity,
emissivity, Taylor–Quinney fraction (0.9). Rolled armour (Tiger plate, Brinell ~260–300),
cast armour, grey cast iron, cast aluminium alloy, mild sheet steel, copper, and the fluids petrol,
engine oil, coolant and air. Bond-based peridynamics fixes Poisson's ratio at 1/4 (steel: 0.29);
named in code and ledger.

## Stage 1 — the layered residual-velocity chain (`fea_damage::chain`)

Input: the authoritative `ImpactEvent` of the struck plate (unchanged), the shot line in the hull
frame, and the ordered layers the line crosses behind the plate: air gaps, each component it
enters (casing wall in, interior, casing wall out — a line can enter and leave one component
several times), crew volumes (targets only), and the far wall. Laws, each named in code:

1. **Struck plate:** the existing `resolve_with_budget_loss_at_speed` result, untouched. Gate: every
   test in `ballistics.rs` and `armor_calibration_tests.rs` passes unchanged.
2. **Further solid layers** (casings, the far wall, solid steel parts): the same calibrated
   capability at the current speed (`pen_flat_at_v`) against the layer's RHA-equivalent thickness
   (areal density over rolled armour's density times a named material resistance factor, times the
   project's own slope law at the layer's obliquity). If capability exceeds it: Lambert–Jonas
   residual speed with the ballistic limit from the same DeMarre exponent, and the momentum share
   the punched plug takes (Recht–Ipson: `a = m/(m + m_plug)`, plug of the layer's thickness at the
   projectile's diameter) in place of the struck plate's fixed 0.92. Otherwise the round stops in
   that layer, at the depth where its capability is spent (capability spent uniformly along the
   path), and lodges there.
3. **Liquid and homogenised interiors:** hydrodynamic drag, `v(x) = v₀ exp(−ρ C_d A x / 2m)`, with
   a named drag coefficient; for homogenised machinery the solid-layer law with the effective
   density instead.
4. **Air:** drag over the gap (negligible over a hull width; computed anyway).
5. **APHE with filler:** the existing behaviour is kept — the burst at the entry, with the existing
   internal blast and fragments. No fuze-delay model is introduced; the chain for such a round ends
   at the burst and the shell body becomes fragments there (stage 3).
6. **Ricochet, shatter, HE blast, HEAT:** existing terminals and rules, unchanged.

Output: the ordered crossings with speed in and out, energy deposited, whether each casing was
perforated or only struck, where the round stopped, and the systems affected. Consequences:
a perforated casing knocks its system out (fault by system: engine stopped, fuel tank holed and
leaking, gearbox holed and losing oil, …); a casing struck but not perforated leaves the system
working with the dent recorded; the residual penetrator's own path through crew volumes is passed
to the existing crew model as one more damaging body with its real mass and speed. A round that
stops in the struck plate deep enough to bury its nose lodges in it (below).

The chain replaces the yes/no inside `resolve_player_penetration` (which modules a penetration
reaches, and whether the round goes on) and gives the enemy path the same per-layer result for
its far wall; it does not change whether the struck plate is perforated.

## Stage 2 — the voxel lattice plate solver (`fea_damage::lattice`)

**Domain.** A local patch of the struck plate, not the whole vehicle: plate-frame axes (the
inward normal, the shot's in-plane direction, their cross product), lateral half-extent of 2.5
calibres plus the oblique exit offset, depth the plate thickness (or, for a round that cannot
reach the back face, its stopping depth plus two calibres, the rest being treated as a rigid
backing — named). Cell size `h` is the largest of: the plate thickness over 8, the calibre over
10, and the size that keeps the particle count under a fixed cap; one constant set for every
preset (presets never change a damage outcome). The plate's discretised thickness differs from
the true thickness by at most `h/2`; every result carries `h`.

**Physics.** Velocity-Verlet explicit dynamics, time step 0.8 × the lattice's stability limit.
Bond stiffness from the bulk modulus for the discrete 18-neighbour set; per-bond plastic stretch
with linear hardening, logarithmic rate dependence and linear thermal softening; failure at a
critical stretch scattered ±8 % per bond by a deterministic hash of the impact state (material
heterogeneity — this is what makes each impact unique, together with the impact point, angle,
speed and any earlier damage under the patch). Outer cells carry a damping ramp so stress waves
leave the patch instead of reflecting (named: a damped clamp standing in for the rest of the
plate). The projectile is a rigid body of the shell's calibre with a nose by shell kind
(capped, pointed, sub-calibre core), moving along the prescribed path with the speed profile the
calibrated law implies (kinetic energy removed uniformly along the path through the plate); it
pushes particles by penalty contact with a named Coulomb friction coefficient. Plastic work and
friction work become heat in the particles they act on (Taylor–Quinney 0.9).

**Outputs** (one geometry revision, published once the solve is complete):

- The plate after the impact: displaced particle positions, broken bonds, temperatures.
- Measured terminal geometry written into `ArmorScar`: aperture across and along the shot, entry
  lip and back-face bulge or scab radius, petals (count, length, measured from the clusters that
  stay attached to the plate around the bore), plug (the detached cluster ahead of the nose),
  crater depth, resistance lost — replacing `terminal_geometry` and `kinetic_crater_mm` for
  kinetic strikes. HE blasts and HEAT keep their existing rules.
- A compact per-scar surface record (front and back displacement on a small polar grid, and the
  aperture outline) for the near-patch renderer; until the renderer consumes it, the record's
  scalar fields carry the shape (stage 2 lands the data, the renderer change waits for a GPU
  check).
- Detached clusters → fragments (stage 3), with their mass, momentum, spin and temperature.
- Ledgers: mass (initial lattice mass = plate particles + detached clusters, exactly, by
  construction), momentum, and energy (projectile work in = elastic energy + plastic work + friction
  heat + fragment kinetic energy + damping loss), with the closure error and the ratio of the work
  the lattice absorbed to the kinetic energy the calibrated law removed.

**Unique, deterministic.** Same impact state, same result, bit for bit (no wall-clock, no thread
order in any sum that matters: per-thread partial sums are combined in a fixed order).

## Stage 3 — fragments, lodging and the hands (`fea_damage::fragments`, `fea_damage::lodged`)

Fragment sources, all mass-conserving:

1. **Plate clusters** from the lattice: the plug and every cluster that broke free. A cluster is
   smaller than one cell only if it is one particle; material finer than the lattice cell is not
   resolved, and a resolved cluster may be split into the existing authored size range (1.4–9 mm
   cubes) by a named sub-cell partition that conserves its mass, momentum and kinetic energy
   (translational plus the velocity spread its particles had when it separated).
2. **The shell body** of an APHE round that bursts inside: its mass minus filler, partitioned into
   the existing authored size distribution, speeds from the existing profile; no fragmentation law
   is introduced. Shatter fragments stay outside the plate as today.
3. **The residual penetrator**, as one body, when the chain lets it go on.

The bodies enter the existing live pool (`HotFragment`, now carrying mass and temperature) and the
existing damage forecast (`resolve_fragment_damage`), so crew, module, ammunition and audio
consumers keep working. The one-online-authority migration of the 2026-09-29 plan (consequences
committed from the fixed-tick contact events instead of the forecast) is the next step after this
branch and is not claimed here. Drag acts on every fragment.

**Lodging.** A fragment lodges where it strikes a surface hard enough to embed: its normal kinetic
energy exceeds the work to push its leading face into the struck material by a quarter of its own
size (flow stress × presented area × depth; named). A lodged fragment is persistent state in its
frame (hull or turret), with mass, size, temperature, embed depth and the force needed to pull it
free (Coulomb friction on the measured grip). Inside the fighting compartment the crewman can take
it in hand (the same hand rules as a loose case: reach, nothing in between, hot steel waits until
it can be held) and throw it, through an open hatch or onto the floor; outside, from the deck. A
fragment that does not lodge comes to rest and stays as loose steel until thrown out.

**Lodged shells.** A kinetic round stopped by the struck plate with its nose buried (stopping
depth at least 0.6 calibre — named) stays in the hole, nose in, at the lattice's final pose. The
pull-out force is the friction on the lattice's final contact forces against the shell. Removal:
by hand if the force is within one man's pull, with the toolbox bar if within its mechanical
advantage, otherwise a workshop job. A round stopped inside a component casing lodges there and is
removed from inside. Lodged rounds are inert: the game's existing fuze behaviour gives a stopped
round no burst.

## Stage 4 — heat, air and pressure (`fea_damage::thermal`, `fea_damage::compartment`)

**Heat.** Particle temperatures from the lattice's plastic and friction work; afterwards the patch
keeps cooling by conduction through intact bonds (explicit diffusion, its own stable step) and by
radiation (emissivity of oxidised steel) and natural convection from exposed cell faces. The
scar's rim temperature for the renderer is the hottest exposed rim particle of that simulation,
replacing the authored 2.5 s exponential. Fragments cool as lumped bodies (radiation and
convection over their area); their glow is the Planck radiance at their temperature, replacing the
authored age stages. The shared Planck function is the one the rim glow already uses.

**Air resistance.** Quadratic drag on every fragment: air density from the physical environment,
a tumbling-cube drag coefficient and mean presented area (a cube's mean projected area is a quarter
of its surface area, Cauchy). Named, with the coefficient's provenance.

**Compartment pressure.** A filled shell bursting inside raises the fighting compartment's mean gas
pressure by the released energy over the compartment's free air volume (ideal gas, γ = 1.4;
quasi-static, no shock-wave model), and the gas vents through the apertures that are actually open:
hatches, ports, holes from the scar record, by choked orifice flow. Pressure acts on hatches
(force on the hatch area against its latch) and is drawn in the cross sections. The crew's existing
blast response is unchanged.

## Stage 5 — cross sections (`fea_damage::section`, `fea-cross-section PREFIX CASE`)

A CPU-only subcommand: it loads the baked Tiger meshes with the CPU glTF loader (no wgpu device),
cuts them with the plane containing the shot line, runs the same chain and lattice the game runs,
and writes a slow-motion frame sequence (about 1/1000 real time) as SVG, plus PNG through
`rsvg-convert` when present: plates and casings in section, the shell, fragments coloured by
temperature, the lattice cells around the hole coloured by temperature, the compartment pressure,
and the names of the systems knocked out at each frame. Case set: 45 mm, 57 mm, 76 mm AP and APCR,
85 mm, 122 mm, a 14.5 mm anti-tank rifle round (a case-set projectile only: no anti-tank rifle fires
in the game yet), HE on the roof, a ricochet; the front lower plate into the transmission, the
hull side into a fuel tank, the turret front, the engine deck. The GPU renders of the same cases
are queued, not claimed.

## Performance and the exhaustion rule

The lattice is the cost. Budget: particle cap and step count fixed per case class, multi-threaded
over a fixed partition with ordered reduction. `fea-bench` (CPU) reports per impact class the wall
time p50/p95/p99 and the maximum on this machine, separately from driving. The solve runs inside
the impact tick; if its wall-time budget is exhausted the damage stays *pending*: the simulation
does not advance past that tick until the solve commits (a visible, logged hitch), so no consumer
ever sees a half-solved plate, nothing is skipped, and both presets get the same result. A test
proves a budget-sliced solve reproduces the unsliced reference bit for bit.

  energy conservatively (the dilatation change of a broken bond is taken into the remaining bonds'

- Tests: `fea_damage::*` (24), all existing `ballistics`, `armor_calibration`, `armor_damage`,

### Stage 3 in play (landed): steel in hand, lodged rounds drawn and removable, pressure on the hatches

- **Fragments that stay.** A body the lattice solved never retires: it cools through the heat
  pages to a fifth, cold oxidised-steel page (below 750 K, no emission) and rests where it
  stopped, on the floor or embedded in a wall (the embedding depth is kept). The fragment ring
  passes over resting solved steel and the chip in hand; only a pool full of resting steel gives
  up its oldest. The authored population still retires with its life.
- **Taking it in hand.** At the loader's station the crosshair picks a resting fragment within
  2.2 m (a ray against a sphere of the chip's size, at least 12 mm). The prompt says whether it is
  too hot (above 343 K) or stuck fast (an embedded chip whose grip beats one man's 400 N pull,
  by the lodged-round friction rule on its cavity-work depth). Held left button takes it; it
  rides the spent case's light spring; releasing lets it fall under the ordinary fragment
  collision; R throws it at 6 m/s. A throw whose line meets no source surface within 3 m goes out
  through an open aperture and leaves the simulation.
- **Lodged rounds drawn.** One exact physical pool per kinetic specification (13), so a lodged
  round casts and occludes in the ray tracing tier, posed with its hull or turret each frame. The
  body is the chain's penetrator: the solve's diameter and nose, a solid-steel cylinder holding
  the round's mass (2.6–3.2 calibres long for the full-calibre rounds); an APCR core is drawn
  three core diameters long (named: the carrier, most of its mass, is not modelled). Six per
  specification are drawn at most (named).
- **Lodged rounds removed.** A new field repair job, worked with the toolbox in hand at the
  middle of the round's exposed length with a clear path: a hand pull 4 s, the bar 15 s (named
  tuning), sledge blows one per hammer swing for the counted blows. Workshop rounds stay. The
  repair lamp ignores lodged rounds (they are not faults).
- **Compartment pressure on the hatches.** A filled round that bursts behind the struck plate
  inside the crew compartments' air raises their quasi-static pressure; the four lids and the
  cupola at their source apertures and the solved entry hole vent it; a closed lid whose 20 kN
  latch the pressure beats is opened. A 76 mm APHE's 65 g gives about 11 kPa (no lid moves); a
  152 mm HE's 5.9 kg throws every closed lid.
- **Saves.** `vehicles.tanks` is owner version 2 (lodged rounds; deferred plate solves, which are
  re-run from their saved problems on load and give the identical answer; the chip in hand) and
  `combat.projectiles` version 2 (fragments carry their solved temperature and embedding). Saves
