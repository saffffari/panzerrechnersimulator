# Ladeschütze (LS): Zeilen für die Stimmprobe

The loader works blind and hard: he hears the fight, he does not see it. His lines are short shouts over the breech, counts of what is left, and the body's noises of lifting, heat and fumes.

## How to read this sheet

- **ID** is stable: position prefix and a number. Gaps are left on purpose for later lines.
- **Wann** is what has just happened in the tank or outside it, in plain words, so a line can be tied to a game event.
- **Int** is intensity, 1 to 5: 1 quiet routine, 2 alert and focused, 3 urgent, 4 under fire or in pain, 5 extremis (panic, agony, fury).
- **Zeile** is the text to send to the voice. Tags in square brackets are in English because the voice model reads them as direction, not as words. Emotion or delivery tags come first; sounds that are not words ([coughs], [exhales], [grunts]) sit where they happen.
- Numbers are written out as words. "Zwo" is the spoken radio form of "zwei". Three dots are a pause. Capitals mark the one word to hit.
- Wording is mine, built from German Army crew vocabulary. It has not been checked against a period manual. The call sign is invented.
- Tags are cues, not guarantees: render several takes of a line and keep the best. One or two tags a line work better than five.
