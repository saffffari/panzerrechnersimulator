# Candidate Tiger I operations for a "survive the battle" reconstruction

Brief, 8 November 2026. Research only: nothing in the game was changed.

## How to read this

- Every figure carries a link. Labels used: **[wiki]** = Wikipedia (useful, but secondary and often thin on citations); **[hobby]** = enthusiast or wargame site; **[archival]** = a source that cites German or Soviet records by archive and roll; **[publisher]** = a bookseller's or publisher's description.
- "Not verified" means I looked and could not confirm it from a source I could open. It does not mean it is false.
- Distances marked "(OSM calc)" are my own straight-line measurements between place coordinates from OpenStreetMap Nominatim. They are estimates of map scale, not historical frontages.
- Several books named here (Schneider, Lochmann/Rubbel/von Rosen, Jentz, Nash, Lawrence, Zetterling and Frankson) were not available to me. I describe them from publisher pages and from how other works cite them, and I say so.

## 1. Kursk, Operation Citadel, July 1943

**Dates.** The offensive opened on 5 July 1943. The wider battle, including the Soviet counter-offensives, ran to 23 August ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk)). Citadel itself lasted about 10 days on the southern face.

**Tiger units and strength.**
- Total: 146 Tigers in two battalions and four divisional companies ([Tank Museum](https://tankmuseum.org/?p=12795)).
- **503 (southern face, III Panzer Corps, Army Detachment Kempf):** 45 Tigers, 42 operational four days before the attack ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/503rd_Heavy_Panzer_Battalion)). One company was attached to each of the 6th, 7th and 19th Panzer Divisions on 4 July, and the whole battalion went to the 6th Panzer Division on 11 July ([hobby](https://www.nevingtonwarmuseum.com/schwere-panzer-abteilungen-503.html)). A forum post gives 33 combat-fit Tigers on 9 July, 14 on 10 July and 23 on 11 July ([hobby, forum](https://forum.axishistory.com/viewtopic.php?p=2246422)).
- **503's losses** depend on what is counted. III Panzer Corps reported 19 of 503's tanks "lost" on 9 July, yet only two Tigers were reported destroyed between 5 and 10 July. The rest were damaged and repairable ([archival, Dupuy Institute, citing NARA T312 R68](https://dupuyinstitute.org/2018/12/04/iii-panzer-corps-tank-loss-reports-9-21-july-1943/)). [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/503rd_Heavy_Panzer_Battalion) gives 3 Tigers lost in the operation and 5 more in the retreat.
- **II SS Panzer Corps companies**, inventory on 4 July: LSSAH 13, Das Reich 14, Totenkopf 15, a total of 42. Operational on 4 July: 12, 12 and 11 (35). On 11 July: 39 to 40 in inventory ([archival, Wheatley 2021, Journal of Intelligence History, Tables 1 to 3, citing NARA T313 R390](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/81739/1/Published_Version.pdf)).
- **Grossdeutschland Tiger company and 505 (northern face): strengths not verified.** One account has 505's "24 serviceable Tigers" leading the 2nd Panzer Division towards Olkhovatka, undated ([hobby](https://warhistory.org/article/breaking-into-the-northern-kursk-salient-ii)).

**Alongside.** Germany committed about 777,000 men and 2,451 tanks and assault guns ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk)). Order of battle: III Panzer Corps (6th, 7th, 19th Panzer Divisions); II SS Panzer Corps; XLVIII Panzer Corps with Grossdeutschland; in the north, 9th Army's XLVII and XLI Panzer Corps ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk_order_of_battle)).

**Opposite.** The Voronezh Front (south, including 7th Guards Army) and the Central Front (north, including 13th Army and 2nd Tank Army) had about 1,300,000 men and 3,600 tanks, excluding the Steppe Front ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk); [wiki OOB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk_order_of_battle)). Soviet engineers laid 503,993 anti-tank mines ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk)). Which Soviet divisions faced 503 on each day: not verified.

**Terrain and season.** High summer on open, rolling steppe cut by river valleys (the Northern Donets) and balkas (gullies), with villages and orchards. Good visibility and long engagement ranges.

**Arc of events (southern face).** III Panzer Corps crossed the Northern Donets near Belgorod on 5 July into deep minefields and defences. I could not find a source tying a specific number of first-day mine losses to 503. In the SS sector, LSSAH reported 20 tanks lost on 5 July, six of them Tigers, and Totenkopf 12, five of them Tigers ([hobby search summary of Dupuy material; not verified at source](https://dupuyinstitute.org/category/kursk/page/12/)). The corps fought slowly north-east, with a large tank battle on 9 July ([Dupuy](https://dupuyinstitute.org/2018/10/03/obscure-major-tank-battle-on-9-july-1943/)). On the night of 11 to 12 July, corps commander Breith led a column of the 6th Panzer Division, supported by 503's Tigers, to seize the Donets crossing at Rzhavets. Prokhorovka was still about 15 km away at the end of 12 July ([hobby](https://warhistory.org/article/after-citadel-part-i)). Belgorod to Rzhavets is about 35 km and Rzhavets to Prokhorovka about 20 km (OSM calc). Citadel was halted, and the Germans withdrew to their start lines.

**Fit.**
- (a) Huge simulation: excellent. This is the best-documented Tiger operation in the East, with daily data on both sides for the southern face (see the records section), Luftwaffe photographs of the battlefield, compact ground and clear phases. Its weakness is the sheer number of vehicles.
- (b) Crew story: good. A 503 crew in a single company attached to an army panzer division, from the Donets crossing to the night move on Rzhavets, has a natural arc. Von Rosen's memoir covers 503 at Kursk (section on memoirs).

**Conduct.** For the formations named here during 5 to 17 July 1943, I found no documented atrocity in the sources I checked. Wikipedia's Kursk and SS-division pages describe none for this operation ([LSSAH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_SS_Panzer_Division_Leibstandarte_SS_Adolf_Hitler), [Totenkopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_SS_Panzer_Division_Totenkopf)). Not verified either way. Specialist trial and Soviet Extraordinary State Commission records would need checking. The same SS divisions had committed documented crimes four months earlier at Kharkov (section 6).

## 2. Battalion 503 on the Manych and at Rostov, January to February 1943

**Dates.** From early January to 22 February 1943, about seven weeks ([hobby](https://warhistory.org/%40msw/article/heavy-tank-battalion-503-with-army-group-don-in-southern-russia)).

**Tiger units and strength.** On 6 January: 20 Tigers (17 operational) and 31 Panzer IIIs (20 operational). On 9 January: 11 Tigers and 12 Panzer IIIs attacked at Vessely, and at the end of the day one Tiger was operational. On 22 February: two Tigers and five Panzer IIIs operational ([hobby](https://warhistory.org/%40msw/article/heavy-tank-battalion-503-with-army-group-don-in-southern-russia); the page cites no source, and its wording reads like a US Army study, possibly Wilbeck). 2./502 was attached from 14 January. It detrained at Proletarskaya on 5 to 6 January and counter-attacked on 8 January with a company of Panzer Regiment 39 ([hobby search summary](https://warhistory.org/?p=4415)). By 20 January, two Tigers had been totally lost and three sent home ([hobby, tiger1.info](https://tiger1.info/unit-page/503-Rostov)).

**Alongside.** LVII Panzer Corps, Army Group Don ([hobby](https://www.nevingtonwarmuseum.com/schwere-panzer-abteilungen-503.html)), and II./Panzergrenadier Regiment 128 on 6 January ([hobby](https://warhistory.org/%40msw/article/heavy-tank-battalion-503-with-army-group-don-in-southern-russia)).

**Opposite.** 2nd Guards Army between the Don and the Manych ([hobby, wargame scenario](https://aslscenarioarchive.com/scenario.php?id=63352)). Soviet strength: not verified.

**Terrain and season.** Mid-winter on flat, frozen steppe along the Manych river line, then the city of Rostov. Proletarsk to Rostov is about 164 km (OSM calc), but the battalion fought mostly at bridges and crossings.

**Arc.** After Stalingrad, the battalion guarded the Manych crossings so that Army Group A could withdraw through Rostov. It once covered 65 km in a day. It then fell back into Rostov, where a light company of Panzer IIIs fought in the north-west of the city on 8 February and from 19 to 22 February. It then withdrew to Taganrog to refit ([hobby](https://warhistory.org/%40msw/article/heavy-tank-battalion-503-with-army-group-don-in-southern-russia)). [Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/503rd_Heavy_Panzer_Battalion) instead has it moving to Kharkov on 11 February and fighting there. These accounts conflict.

**Fit.**
- (a) Huge simulation: poor. The ground is enormous and thinly held, the Tiger numbers are tiny, and documentation is weak.
- (b) Crew story: interesting. A rearguard in a retreat, with a mixed Tiger/Panzer III unit and new, unreliable tanks: one Tiger is said to have survived 227 anti-tank rifle hits and 25 gun hits and still driven 60 km ([hobby](https://warhistory.org/%40msw/article/heavy-tank-battalion-503-with-army-group-don-in-southern-russia)). The primary sources are thin.

**Conduct.** None found for 503 in this operation. Not verified.

## 3. Korsun-Cherkassy pocket relief, January to February 1944

**Dates.** The Soviet attack opened on 24 January. The pocket closed around 28 January to 1 February. The breakout began on the night of 16 to 17 February ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Korsun%E2%80%93Cherkassy_pocket)). About four weeks in all.

**Tiger units and strength.** Heavy Panzer Regiment Bäke had 34 Tigers (from 503) and 46 Panthers (from II./Panzer Regiment 23), plus self-propelled artillery and an armoured engineer battalion ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_B%C3%A4ke); [hobby](https://www.armedconflicts.com/16233-Heavy-Tank-Regiment-Bake)). Day-by-day strength: not verified.

**Alongside.** III Panzer Corps had 201 tanks and XLVII Panzer Corps 58 ([wiki infobox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Korsun%E2%80%93Cherkassy_pocket)). Inside the pocket were nearly 60,000 men of Gruppe Stemmermann, including 5th SS Wiking ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Korsun%E2%80%93Cherkassy_pocket)).

**Opposite.** The 1st Ukrainian Front (Vatutin) and the 2nd Ukrainian Front (Konev), including the 5th Guards Tank Army and 6th Tank Army. Combined, 336,700 men and 524 tanks, plus 400 more as reinforcements ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Korsun%E2%80%93Cherkassy_pocket)).

**Terrain and season.** The thaw: deep mud, the Gniloy Tikich river about 15 m wide and 2 m deep with meltwater, slippery slopes, then snow during the breakout. Hill 239 lies at about 49°15.4′N 30°49.4′E, beside Lysyanka ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Korsun%E2%80%93Cherkassy_pocket)). From Shenderivka (Shanderovka, "Hell's Gate") to Lysyanka is about 17 km (OSM calc).

**Arc.** III Panzer Corps drove east from 11 February and reached the Gniloy Tikich with only a small bridgehead. Bäke's regiment ran out of fuel and halted at Hill 239. T-34s of the 5th Guards Tank Army held the hill, so the breaking-out columns had to pass round it under fire and wade the river. About 1,450 non-walking wounded were left in Shanderovka ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Korsun%E2%80%93Cherkassy_pocket)). Estimates of the number who escaped include:
- 27,703 Germans and 1,063 auxiliaries escaped unhurt (German post-battle figures);
- 40,423 escapees including wounded flown out (German official documents);
- "roughly two out of three" (Nash), against "almost one third" dead or wounded (Zetterling and Frankson);
- Soviet claims of about 55,000 dead and 18,000 prisoners;
- Glantz judges the German claims of about 30,000 escaped less credible than the Soviet accounts.

All of these figures are as reported by [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Korsun%E2%80%93Cherkassy_pocket).

**Fit.**
- (a) Huge simulation: strong drama and compact ground, but the Tigers are the relief force outside the pocket. The "survive" goal fits the men inside the pocket, not the Tiger commander.
- (b) Crew story: good. A Tiger crew stuck at Hill 239 without fuel, watching the breakout stream past, is a strong situation.

**Conduct.** An account that Wikipedia attributes to Erickson describes Soviet tanks crushing men on the road and Cossack cavalry sabring Germans who had raised their hands. It also describes attacks on medical columns marked with the Red Cross ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Korsun%E2%80%93Cherkassy_pocket)). For Bäke's regiment, I found no documented atrocity. Not verified.

## 4. Kamenets-Podolsky pocket (Hube's pocket), March to April 1944

**Dates.** The wider offensive ran from 4 March to 17 April. The pocket existed from 23 March to 10 April 1944 ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamenets-Podolsky_pocket)).

**Tiger units and strength** ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamenets-Podolsky_pocket); not verified against Schneider):
- **503**: 24 operational and 10 in repair on 1 March. It was "bled white" by the breakout and withdrawn to Ohrdruf in May.
- **509**: 28 operational on 1 March; one operational at the breakout on 9 April.
- **LSSAH Tiger company**: lost most of its Tigers. Its report attributes 75% of losses to demolition to prevent capture, 15% to combat and 10% to other causes. On 7 April, LSSAH had 2 Tigers operational.

[Wiki, 503](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/503rd_Heavy_Panzer_Battalion) says only that the battalion was trapped and lost most of its tanks.

**Alongside.** 1st Panzer Army: about 200,000 ration strength, with only 43 tanks and 50 assault guns on the eve of 4 March (Frieser, via [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamenets-Podolsky_pocket)). II SS Panzer Corps came from France and attacked from the west on 4 April ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamenets-Podolsky_pocket)).

**Opposite.** On 1 March the 1st Ukrainian Front (Zhukov) had 1,409 operational tanks and self-propelled guns. It included the 1st, 3rd Guards and 4th Tank Armies ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamenets-Podolsky_pocket)).

**Terrain and season.** The thaw (rasputitsa) and fuel shortages, across the rivers of Podolia (Zbruch, Seret, Strypa). Kamenets-Podolsky to Buchach is about 97 km (OSM calc). The pocket marched west.

**Arc.** The pocket formed and moved as a whole. The breakout began on 28 March, westward and against the expected direction, and linked up with the II SS Panzer Corps on 10 April. In the pocket, 1st Panzer Army lost 322 tanks, most of its artillery and 22,389 motor vehicles ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamenets-Podolsky_pocket)).

**Fit.**
- (a) Huge simulation: the "survive" goal fits perfectly (get out with the tank), but the moving pocket covers roughly 100 km and is very hard to reconstruct hour by hour from what I found.
- (b) Crew story: excellent premise. The fuel runs out, the tank is blown up, and the crew walks out. I found no first-hand Tiger account of it.

**Conduct.** None found for these units in this operation. Not verified.

## 5. Battalion 502 at Leningrad, Nevel, Narva and Dünaburg, 1942 to 1944

**Dates.**
- Mga, 29 August 1942 ([Tank Museum](https://tankmuseum.org/?p=7860));
- Iskra, 13 to 18 January 1943 ([Tank Archives](https://www.tankarchives.com/2023/04/heavy-trophies-from-leningrad.html));
- Lake Ladoga, July to September 1943; Nevel, November to December 1943; Narva, February to April 1944; Pleskau (Pskov), April to May 1944; Dünaburg, July 1944 ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/502nd_Heavy_Panzer_Battalion)).

**Tiger units and strength.** Four Tigers in August 1942. Full strength (45) was reached after 31 Tigers were shipped in May 1943. In all, 105 Tiger Is and 8 Tiger IIs were issued ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/502nd_Heavy_Panzer_Battalion)). The 1st Company had only 8 Tigers during Iskra ([Tank Archives](https://www.tankarchives.com/2023/04/heavy-trophies-from-leningrad.html)). The battalion claimed 1,400 tanks and 2,000 guns for 107 own losses, a ratio of 13.08 (Wilbeck, pp. 186 and 188, via [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/502nd_Heavy_Panzer_Battalion)). These are German claims.

**Alongside / opposite.** Army Group North, 18th Army (siege line); Soviet Leningrad and Volkhov Fronts. Strengths for each phase: not verified.

**Terrain and season.** Forest, peat bog and swamp, with causeways and peat workings. Tiger 100 was lost in a peat pit ([Tank Archives](https://www.tankarchives.com/2023/04/heavy-trophies-from-leningrad.html)). Mga to Sinyavino is about 17 km (OSM calc). At Narva: a river line, bridgeheads and forest. The battles were fought in every season, including deep winter.

**Arc.** A disastrous debut, then piecemeal company actions around the Leningrad bottleneck. Then the long retreat: Nevel, the Narva bridgehead battles of 1944, and the July 1944 fighting near Dünaburg (Daugavpils), where Carius was wounded. The Malinava action belongs to this last phase. I found no source for the details of the Malinava claim and have left them out.

**Fit.**
- (a) Huge simulation: poor to medium. Bogs and forest suit a small, close game better than a large one, and the force data I found is thin.
- (b) Crew story: excellent, because Carius's memoir is the best-known first-person Tiger narrative, organised around these exact places.

**Conduct.** The battalion fought on the siege line around Leningrad. Some historians describe the siege, with about 1,042,000 Soviet civilian deaths, as a racially motivated starvation policy amounting to genocide (Ganzenmüller; Barber and Dzeniskevich, via [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad)). That is a crime of the German leadership and the besieging army as a whole. I found no documented atrocity attributed to 502 itself. Not verified.

## 6. Third Battle of Kharkov, February to March 1943

**Dates.** 19 February to 15 March 1943, about 3.5 weeks; Belgorod was retaken around 17 to 18 March ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Kharkov)).

**Tiger units.** Tiger companies of the SS divisions and Grossdeutschland. Totenkopf received its Tiger company in February 1943 ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_SS_Panzer_Division_Totenkopf)). The Kharkov article gives no Tiger count ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Kharkov)). Whether 503 took part is disputed: compare [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/503rd_Heavy_Panzer_Battalion) with [hobby](https://warhistory.org/%40msw/article/heavy-tank-battalion-503-with-army-group-don-in-southern-russia). Counts: not verified.

**Alongside.** About 350 German tanks; the SS Panzer Corps of about 20,000 men; XLVIII and the First Panzer Army's corps ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Kharkov)).

**Opposite.** The Voronezh and Southwestern Fronts, Popov's Mobile Group, 3rd Tank Army and others. About 346,000 men defended Kharkov after the counterstroke began. The infobox's "c. 59 tanks" for the Soviet side looks unreliable, and the article flags its own Soviet tank numbers as missing ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Kharkov)).

**Terrain and season.** Late winter turning to thaw; steppe and river lines (Samara, Mosh), then street fighting in a big city. Krasnograd to Kharkov is about 89 km, and Kharkov to Belgorod about 72 km (OSM calc).

**Arc.** Manstein's counterstroke cut off Popov's group. The SS Panzer Corps then went for Kharkov directly, contrary to orders to encircle it, and took the city after house-to-house fighting from 11 to 15 March ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Kharkov)).

**Fit.**
- (a) Huge simulation: medium. It is a war of movement over a large area, and the Tigers are scattered in companies.
- (b) Crew story: medium. Winter street fighting is distinctive, but the formations involved carry the crimes below.

**Conduct.**
- LSSAH troops murdered wounded Soviet soldiers in Kharkov's military hospital, "several hundred" of them, and routinely executed captured officers and commissars (Ripley, p. 73, via [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_SS_Panzer_Division_Leibstandarte_SS_Adolf_Hitler)).
- In February 1943, Peiper's battalion earned the name "Blowtorch Battalion" after the inhabitants of two villages were shot or burned. LSSAH troops killed 872 men, women and children at Yefremovka and Semyonovka, about 240 of them burned in a church (Parker 2014, pp. 356 to 357, via [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_SS_Panzer_Division_Leibstandarte_SS_Adolf_Hitler)).

A game set here with an SS Tiger crew would have to face these crimes.

**General warning on claims.** Kill claims attached to individual commanders are rarely reliable (Neitzel 2002; Zaloga 2015, via [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_B%C3%A4ke)). Kurowski's popular "panzer aces" books include fiction; for example he credits Bäke with 32 tanks in one engagement (Smelser and Davies 2008, via [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_B%C3%A4ke)). The error runs both ways: Soviet claims for German tank losses at Prokhorovka far exceed what German records show ([Wheatley 2021](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/81739/1/Published_Version.pdf)). German "losses" in daily reports include repairable damage ([Dupuy](https://dupuyinstitute.org/2018/12/04/iii-panzer-corps-tank-loss-reports-9-21-july-1943/)).

## Recommendation (my judgement, separate from the facts above)

**(a) Huge "survive the battle" simulation**

1. **Kursk, southern face, 503 with III Panzer Corps, 5 to 15 July 1943.** It is the only candidate where three things already exist and are published:
   - daily strength and loss records for both sides (Lawrence; Wheatley; NARA roll numbers);
   - wartime aerial photographs of the ground;
   - a diary-based memoir from inside the battalion (von Rosen).

   The ground is compact (about 35 km deep) and the season is clear. The cost is vehicle count: hundreds of tanks on each side within the map.
2. **Korsun relief, 11 to 17 February 1944.** It has a tight map, a dramatic structure and a clear objective. But the Tiger is outside the pocket, so "survive" must be reframed as "hold the corridor at Hill 239 with no fuel".
3. **Hube's pocket.** It has the most honest "survive" premise (escape with the tank, or destroy it and walk). But it is about 100 km of moving front with thin day-level sources found so far.

Kharkov, the Manych and the Leningrad sector rank lower for this mode.

**(b) Small crew story**

1. **502 at Narva in 1944, built around Carius.** It has the richest first-person narrative and close terrain that suits a few vehicles. Treat his claims as claims, and cross-check against Pamyat Naroda before using them as scripted events.
2. **One 503 company at Kursk, from the Donets crossing to Rzhavets**, using von Rosen's diary-based account for the hour-by-hour layer.
3. **A 503 rearguard on the Manych, January 1943**: a mixed Tiger/Panzer III unit, new machines failing, a retreat. Good story, weakest sources.

For both modes, Kursk-South with 503 is the strongest single choice. It is also the one where the owner's rule on atrocities is easiest to honour truthfully: III Panzer Corps was an army formation, and I found no documented crime for it in this operation. The SS divisions' Tiger companies fought beside it, and they carry the documented Kharkov crimes of four months earlier; the game should not blur that line.
