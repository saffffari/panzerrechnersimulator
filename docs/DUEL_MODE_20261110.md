# The duel: two players, Tiger against Tiger (10 November 2026)

Status: **version 1 works between two processes on one machine; not yet played by two people,
not yet tried between two machines or over the internet.**

## What it is

Two players on the Canyon map, each commanding a Tiger with the living crew. No T-34s, guns or
infantry. First Tiger out of action loses.

## How to start it

```sh
tools/launch_demo_20261101.sh --duel-host                    # waits for the other player (UDP 28944)
tools/launch_demo_20261101.sh --duel-join 192.168.1.20       # the host's address (":PORT" optional)
```

The same through the environment: `KESSEL_DUEL=host`, `host:PORT`, `join:ADDRESS[:PORT]`.
Over the internet the joiner needs to reach the host's UDP port: a forwarded port on the host's
router, or both machines on one VPN (Tailscale and the like). There is no lobby or relay yet.

## How it works (`src/duel.rs`)

- Each machine runs its own whole game: its own Tiger, crew, damage, fire, repairs.
- Thirty times a second each sends the other its tank's pose (hull, turret, gun), whether it is
  still in action, and its last six main-gun shots (shell, muzzle position, direction, seed).
- The other Tiger is drawn from those poses (the one ally rig the scene builds for a duel).
- Each of the other player's shots is fired here as a real shell from his muzzle. What it does
  to my tank is decided here, by the ordinary damage model. My shots are decided on his machine.
- A tank reports itself out of action; the other side then shows it destroyed and the result.

Named consequence: a hit is judged on the victim's machine, where the victim is. With delay
between the two, a shot that looked on target on the shooter's screen can miss a moving tank.
Nobody is killed by a shot his own screen showed missing.

## Checked

`panzerrechnersystem duel-run host:28951 PREFIX 70` and `duel-run join:127.0.0.1:28951 PREFIX 70`
as two processes, starts 150 m apart (`KESSEL_DUEL_SEPARATION_M=150`), a fixture laying and
firing every six seconds: both ends connected, about 30 packets a second each way, every shot
sent was received once, the host's second PzGr 39 penetrated the guest's hull front at 135 m and
set off its ammunition; the guest reported "lost", the host "won". Unit tests cover the packet
format, the request parsing, the two starts and a loopback exchange.

## Not in version 1

- The crew's gunner does not engage the other Tiger by himself: the player lays and fires (or
  designates; untested against the other Tiger).
- Machine guns, smoke, fire, wreck state and crew on foot are not sent. The other Tiger's
  damage is not shown beyond the marks my own shells leave and its final destruction.
- The two starts are 700 m apart along the course's route; nothing yet checks that they are fair.
- No reconnection, no lobby, no relay server, no protection against a modified client.
- A result line is drawn at the bottom left; there is no end card or rematch.
