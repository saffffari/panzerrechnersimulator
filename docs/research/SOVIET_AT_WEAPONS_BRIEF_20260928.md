# Soviet anti-tank threats to a Tiger I, winter 1942–43 (research brief, 28 Sep 2026)

Tags: [D] documented, [I] inference; confidence H/M/L. Compiled for the game's anti-tank guns and
exterior damage. Main finding: **no Tigers fought at Stalingrad or in the relief attempt**; the
nearest real case is s.Pz.Abt. 503 (with the former 2./502) on the Manych–Rostov axis,
Jan–Feb 1943, which is the evidence base here.

## Tiger I armour [D-H]
Nose 100 mm at ~25°; driver's plate 100 mm at 9°; glacis 60 mm near-horizontal. Upper hull side
80 mm vertical; **lower hull side 60 mm** (Soviets measured 62–63), mostly behind the interleaved
road wheels. Rear 80 mm; roof and floor 25 mm. Turret front 100 mm; mantlet ~100–110 mm (to ~200
where it overlaps); turret sides/rear 80 mm; roof 25 mm (40 mm only from March 1944).

## Organisation and crews [D-H]
- Rifle regiment (04/300, Jul 1942): 6 × 45 mm, 4 × 76 mm regimental, 27 AT rifles; division
  AT battalion 12 × 45 mm. From 04/550 (10 Dec 1942) each rifle battalion regains a 2 × 45 mm
  platoon; division 48 × 45 mm, 212 AT rifles.
- Crews were **artillerymen organic to rifle units**; NKO Order 0528 (1 Jul 1942) put them on a
  special register, paid 1.5×/2×, and 500 roubles to gun commander and gunner per tank knocked out.
  Riflemen crewing a gun is anecdotal: emergency only, reduced skill.
- 45 mm crew: commander, gunner, loader, ammunition handler, 2 bearers (+2 drivers, 4 horses off
  the gun). 560 kg firing; 60° traverse; 25–30 rounds/min maximum; moved by hand up to ~500 yd.
  ZiS-3: 1,116 kg, crew 7, 54° traverse.

## Penetration against the Tiger (winter 1942–43 ammunition)
Nominal: 45 mm BR-240 43 mm at 500 m (90°); APCR BR-240P 88 mm at 100 m, 66 mm at 500 m (APCR
scarce: "on special account, issued one round at a time"). ZiS-3 BR-350A 82/75/67 mm at
100/500/1000 m. PTRD 40 mm at 100 m. RPG-40/41 20–25 mm.

Kubinka, 25–30 Apr 1943, captured Tiger #121 [D-H]: 45 mm APCR penetrated the 80 mm side at 200 m
(1 of 2) and the 60 mm lower side at 350 m; M-42 AP failed against the side even at 100 m. **F-34
76 mm: no penetration even at 200 m.** 85 mm 52-K: front at 1,000 m. 122 mm A-19: through front
and out the rear; a turret hit tore it off its ring.

## Effects of non-penetrating hits [D unless marked]
Vision block lost and temporary blinding; MGs destroyed; gun mount and recoil damage from 76 mm
mantlet hits; road wheels, swing arms and idler damaged; tracks — a 45 mm hit at 30–200 m breaks
one, AT rifles need 4–5 hits on one pin ("senseless"), one grenade under a track immobilises and
two cut it; sprocket destroyed by 45 mm APCR; engine fire when the exhaust shield is shot away;
fuel leak from cracked welds; floor dented by one AT grenade, penetrated by two; a bottle on an
intake is sucked in with the engine running; the automatic extinguisher (7 s) can put it out.
Turret jam from ring hits: a Soviet claim, keep it uncommon [I].

## Tactics [D]
Sited for **flanking fire** from ambush behind obstacles and mines ("fire pockets"); open suddenly
when tanks reach the obstacles; the Red Star rule: open at 500–600 yd and not before, 12–14 shots
in the ~2 minutes a tank needs to close, 20–25% hits, 2–3 tanks per gun before it is destroyed.
Best direct-fire range 600–700 m for divisional guns, 300–400 m for regimental. Aim at flanks and
rear, tracks, fuel tanks, the turret ring. AT rifles effective to 500 m, against Tigers only
≤150 m; Chuikov's riflemen let tanks close to 50–100 m. Manych, 13 Jan 1943: a battery firing all
4 guns at 2 probing tanks was overrun from the flank — engage with one gun and let them come.
If overrun, crews go to ground and re-man the guns after the tanks pass. Aiming points on a Tiger
(1943 pamphlets): drive sprocket, lower side over the fuel tanks, vision slits and cupola, the
hull MG ball mount, engine-deck intakes (grenades, bottles), the 10 mm turret gap.
Town fighting: guns in rooms and wall breaches, low in half-basement windows; many alternate
positions, moved at night.

## Design recommendation (the brief's synthesis)
Common: AT rifle teams and 45 mm 53-K guns (2 per battalion, 6 per regiment). Occasional: ZiS-3
in direct fire; 76 mm regimental. Rare set pieces: an 85 mm AA battery, a 122 mm howitzer, an A-19.
Leave out ZiS-2, 76 mm APCR, RPG-43, 122 mm HEAT. AI: concealed until ~300–400 m (45 mm),
500–600 m (76 mm), ≤150 m (AT rifles), ≤25 m (grenadiers); prefer flank and rear shots, else
tracks, sprocket, vision devices and MGs; one gun engages a probing tank; relocate by hand after
revealing; go to ground if the Tiger closes under 100 m and re-man after it passes; 0–3 APCR per
gun, only for side/rear ≤200 m (lower hull ≤350 m).
