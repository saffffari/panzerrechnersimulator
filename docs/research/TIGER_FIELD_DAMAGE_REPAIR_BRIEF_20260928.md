# Early Tiger I, winter 1942–43: component damage and field repair (sourced brief)

**Scope.** This brief covers "initial/early" Tiger I production: Fgst. 250001 to about 250250, with the HL 210 P45 engine, the drum cupola and rubber-tyred wheels. These are the tanks used by s.Pz.Abt. 502 near Leningrad from August 1942 and by s.Pz.Abt. 503 (joined by 2./502, later 3./503) on the Don, Manych and Rostov front from January to March 1943. When the only evidence comes from a later Tiger, the entry says **(later Tiger)**. The Tigerfibel is dated 1 Aug 1943, so it came out *after* this winter, but it draws on that winter's experience. It already describes the 700 PS HL 230.

**Tags.** **[P]** = period document (combat report, manual, trial report). **[M]** = first-hand memoir written after the war. **[S]** = serious secondary source (Jentz/Doyle, Schneider, archive-based researchers). **[F]** = folklore, forum or unverified. **(inf.)** = my own inference from the cited evidence. "via X" means the primary text is known to me only through X's transcription.

## 0. The "227 hits" Tiger: where the story comes from

**Published origin: TF p. 4 [P]** ("Er hält allerhand aus…"):
> "Dieser Tiger erhielt im Südabschnitt in 6 Stunden: 227 Treffer Panzerbüchse, 14 Treffer 5,2 cm und 11 Treffer 7,62 cm. Keiner ging durch. Laufrollen und Verbindungsstücke waren zerschossen, 2 Schwingarme arbeiteten nicht mehr, mehrere Pak-Treffer saßen genau auf der Kette, und auf 3 Minen war er gefahren. Er fuhr mit eigener Kraft noch 60 km Gelände."

The print really does say **"5,2 cm"**. The Soviets had no gun of that calibre. The familiar "14 × 57 mm" is a later tidying-up.

**Underlying document: Lt. Zabel's report** [P via S: ZR-S; ZR-A].
- **What it is.** An *Erfahrungsbericht* sent up to the Generalinspekteur der Panzertruppen in early to mid 1943.
- **Unit.** Zabel belonged to **3./s.Pz.Abt. 503**. That company was the former **2./s.Pz.Abt. 502**, which was issued 9 Tigers shortly before Christmas 1942, attached to 503 on 14 Jan 1943, and redesignated 3./503 on 10 Feb (Jentz) or 22 Feb 1943 (Schneider) [S: HAM from J-CT/Schneider; T1].
- **Date and place.** 10–11 Feb 1943, in an attack by **Kampfgruppe Sander** on the collective farm west of **Ssemernikowo**. The attack lasted more than 6 hours [P via S]. "Ssemernikowo" is Semernikovo (Семерниково), just west of Rostov-on-Don. That identification is my inference from the transliteration; check it against a 1943 map.
- **Tank.** Usually identified as **Tiger "231"** [S: T1, TE]. The identification rests on photographs, not on the report. The AHF thread notes there were two different "231"s: one in 2./503, and one that came from 2./502 [F].

**Hit count.**
- ZR-A: "227 hits by AT rifles, 14 hits by 5.7 cm AT guns and 11 hits by 7.62 cm AT guns."
- ZR-S: "14 hits from 5.7 cm **and 4.5 cm**". The report itself lists two 4.5 cm hits on the cupola.
- Soviet ZiS-2 57 mm production stopped on 1 Dec 1941 and the gun returned to service only on 15 Jun 1943 [S: WP ZiS-2; TA]. So many of the "5.7 cm" hits were probably 45 mm (inf.).

**Damage, in the report's order** (the two translations merged; where they differ, both are given):
1. A 7.62 cm hit on the driver's front plate tore off the spare track links, which were held there by an iron/steel bar. The crew felt a dull clang and a slight jolt.
2. AT-rifle hits showed as "a lighter bang followed by a burst of yellow smoke" (ZR-A). ZR-S describes "a shimmering yellow flash inside the fighting compartment" with each hit.
3. A 4.5 cm hit on the cupola smashed the brackets of the glass vision block. The block "jammed and became opaque" from the heat (ZR-A); ZR-S says it was "welded tight". The commander's view in that sector was gone.
4. A further hit destroyed the brackets of the cupola hatch, which "fell down into the fighting compartment". A wave of heat and acrid smoke followed.
5. The loader's hatch, stuck half-open, took several AT-rifle hits that knocked off its hinges and brackets. After the battle it "could be opened only with the aid of a crowbar" (ZR-S).
6. Hits counted on the cupola after the battle: 2 × 4.5 cm and 15 AT rifle.
7. Both machine guns were shot to pieces.
8. The smoke dischargers were hit and went off. The smoke inside was so thick that the Tiger was "for a short time completely out of action".
9. A 7.62 cm hit on the mantlet sheared the gun-mounting bolts. The recoil brake lost its fluid and the gun stayed at full recoil. ZR-A adds that "due to electric problems the breech block could not be shut".
10. Shock from further hits put the radio out. ZR-A says the steering levers jammed; ZR-S says the radio, "a gas tube" (sic) and the driver's gear lever were damaged.
11. The engine caught fire when the exhaust cover was shot away. The fire-fighting system put it out (ZR-A); ZR-S says it was put out "at once".
12. ZR-S only: "An explosive charge thrown on top of the Tiger from the side" was felt as a dull explosion with heat and smoke.
13. ZR-A only: hits "loosened some turret ring screws. The turret traversing system failed temporarily."
14. Running gear: the right side was heavily damaged.
    - ZR-S: "Several road wheels and their suspension arms were perforated. The idler wheel had worked itself out of its mount."
    - ZR-A: "The connecting pieces for several running wheels were ruined, two torsion bars were broken. A rear idler wheel bearing was damaged."
    - The tracks were hit several times but mobility was "not particularly impaired".
15. The hits cracked several weld seams, and a fuel tank started leaking.
16. The crew felt no hunger or thirst, and the 6-hour attack "had gone by in a flash".

**What the tank did afterwards.**
- It drove **60 km further under its own power** [P].
- It then went back to the unit and was shipped to Germany; a photo shows it on transport tracks [F: AHF]. T1 says it was "retired from service and got mentioned in the Tiger Fibel handbook" [S].
- A claim that its turret later became a training pillbox at Paderborn is **[F]**.

### Recovery, and whether one Tiger could tow another
- **Tigerfibel p.44 [P]:** "Notfalls kann Dir ein Tiger-Kamerad flottmachen, aber vermeide es. Unternimm […] keine selbständigen Versuche weiterzukommen. Du quälst Motor und Triebwerk, es nützt doch nichts." ("If need be a comrade's Tiger can get you going, but avoid it.") The crew prepares the tank; the *Bergezug* tows it "im Tandemzug". The page diagram shows 2 tractors in tandem, and 4 tractors winching a Tiger out of a river.
- **Jentz** (J-CT, via Fletcher/TM) [S]: towing Tiger with Tiger "was not condoned, except in an emergency, since the sophisticated transmission system wouldn't stand it". The usual method was 2, sometimes 3, 18 t Famos coupled together.
- **Other secondary sources:**
  - WP citing Newsome 2020 [S]: towing was officially forbidden because it caused thrown tracks, damaged sprockets, bent swing arms and engine overheating.
  - Carruthers [S]: the towing tank's engine overheated, sometimes to breakdown or fire, and the order was "routinely disobeyed".
  - Fletcher [S]: two Tigers towing one "often resulted in the loss of all three due to gearbox failure".
- **What crews actually did** [P via S: TM]. An Allied POW interrogation (undated) reports it was "customary to dismantle the flexible couplings in the half shaft drives and tow it … by another Tiger", with two ropes crossed in an X. A Tiger whose track had ridden over the sprocket needed two Tigers in tandem.
- **The distinction the manual draws.** TF p.39 accepts short pulls out of a bog: "Ein anderer Tiger zieht Dich heraus. Seil verankern, Haken in die Gleiskette, selbst herausziehen!" Long tows were the problem (inf.).
- **Famo numbers.** An Sd.Kfz. 9 was rated to tow 28 t, so 2–4 were needed per Tiger [S: WP]. The 502 maintenance report reproduced in Carius's appendix says 3–4 × 18 t prime movers, tow bars too weak, and 5 uphill [M/P via review].

## 2. Tigerfibel (D 656/27, 1 Aug 1943): procedures, quoted with printed page numbers

### 2.1 Breaking and rejoining track; running gear
- **p.35, tension:** "Kettenspannung ist außerordentlich wichtig! … 4 Finger breit soll sie über der ersten Laufrolle durchhängen. Beachte beim Spannen die Anschläge und überdrehe sie nicht. Sonst muß der Motor raus."
- **p.35, fitting the combat track:** "Flansche tadellos von Farbe, Rost, Schmutz und Eis säubern und hauchdünn fetten, Räder aufsetzen, Schrauben kreuzweise gut festziehen und sichern. Verladekette unter dem Leitrad auf einer Seite aufmachen, Panzer vorfahren, bis Kette abgespult ist, Geländekette davor auslegen, Panzer vorfahren, bis Kettenende dicht vor der ersten Laufrolle liegt, Seil 3mal um das Triebrad schlingen, Kette anseilen, das andere Triebrad mit dem Lenkhebel festbremsen, Kette aufziehen, Kette schließen und spannen, die andere Seite in gleicher Weise auflegen." The transport track goes on the same way.
- **p.35, links and pins:** "Bolzen und Kettenglieder wechselt man unter dem Leit- oder Triebrad. Neue Glieder nicht zusammenhängend einbauen, sondern verteilen." / "Zahnkränze der Triebräder wechseln, wenn die Vorwärtsflanken abgenutzt sind."
- **p.35, suspension:** "Achte auf gelockerte oder gerissene Bandagen, auf entsicherte Bolzen, gerissene Radscheiben, auf gebrochene Drehstäbe und Kurbeln …"
- **p.36, wheel-change table.** Lifting arms 1–3: "Auflaufbock vor innerstes Laufrad des zu hebenden Armes legen, Pz auffahren". Arms 4–5: "Kette aufmachen. Mit Winden eine Pz-Seite über Kettenzahnhöhe hochheben. Am besten mit 2 soliden Stützplatten, 2 Ölhebern zu 30 t eine Längsseite hochbocken." Wheels to remove per row: 1 / 3 / 4 / 8 / 13; outer flange 1, inner flange 3, idler 3, sprocket 5.
- **p.36, moral:** "Wenn's finster wie in einer Kuh, kalt, naß und dreckig noch dazu, im Matsch versunken Bock und Winden, Hammer und Schlüssel nicht zu finden, wenn Stäbe brechen, Arme hängen, drei Rollen fehlen, fünfe zwängen …"
- **p.37:** drawings of the ramp block, oil jack, and wheel, flange, idler and sprocket removal (puller "7a Abziehen / 7b Aufsetzen").
- **p.39, ice:** "Kettenglied vor die Kette werfen, Schwungfahren, nicht lenken, Kanten senkrecht anfahren." **Mines:** "Spurfahren, auf Spur zurückstoßen, nicht lenken, wenn möglich räumen."
- **p.45, rail loading:** "…lege die Verladekette auf, verstaue die Zusatzlaufrollen …"; the combat tracks are dragged onto the wagon under the tank.

### 2.2 Replacing vision blocks
**The Tigerfibel has no vision-block replacement procedure.** I checked all 92 pages. The nearest items are the driver's-visor jam fix (p.40) and the optics caps and boresighting (p.79). The procedure should be in **D 656/22** (the turret manual listed on TF p.10), which I could not access. The holder mechanism and spares count come from T1 (§1 table).

### 2.3 Winter (frozen interleaved wheels, snow)
- **p.35:** "Im Winter müssen sich alle Laufrollen drehen. Taue sie mit der Lötlampe auf. Sonst verlierst Du die Bandagen."
- **p.43:** "Im Winter nicht auf den blanken Boden abstellen. Reisig, Bretter, dick Stroh, Bohlen, Holzwände oder Zäune unterlegen; Brocken, Eis, Matsch zwischen den Laufrädern hervorholen, alle 2 Stunden etwas bewegen, besonders gefährlich ist abwechselnd Wärme (tauen) und Frost (frieren). Sonst friert er an!" Also: "Lege den Gang ein, mit dem Du losfahren willst. Ein kaltes Wechselgetriebe läßt sich nicht schalten. Klemme den Kupplungsfußhebel so nach unten, daß die Kupplung gelöst bleibt und nicht zusammenfriert."
- **p.39, snow:** "Trockener Neuschnee bis 70 cm ist unbedenklich. Papp und Harsch, Bruchharsch nur bis zur Bodenfreiheit — 50 cm."
- **p.40, driver's visor:** "Fahrersehklappe gängig halten! Im Winter und bei Beschuß klemmt sie manchmal. Löse die 4 versenkten Schrauben am Gehäuse, entferne den Deckel und drehe die außermittigen Buchsen so weit nach links, bis das Räderspiel groß genug ist."
- **p.17, batteries:** "Ein geladener Sammler zerfriert erst bei —65°, ein entladener aber schon bei —10°." / "Im Winter mit Schwunganlasser durchdrehen und starten, das ist billiger als Sammler ausbauen und aufladen!"
- **p.18–19, coolant.** 120 l. Ice expands 10%, so 120 l become 132 l, and "Eis sprengt Felsen und Eisen". Glysantin 2:3: to −20°, 48 l + 72 l water; to −40°, 72 l + 48 l.
- **p.22, starting in cold:** "Obwohl der Tiger bis —20° auch mit dem elektr. Anlasser sofort anspringt, mit dem Schwungkraftanlasser durchdrehen und anwerfen. Schone die Sammler, sonst kannst Du nicht anlassen, wenn's draußen schießt."
- **p.22, warming one Tiger from another:** "Bei großer Kälte kann ein Tiger den anderen anwärmen. Das heiße Kühlwasser des einen Motors wird in den kalten Motor gepumpt …" The warm engine runs at 2,400, then 2,000 rpm until the cold one reaches 50°.
- **p.25, oil:** below −30°, drain 4 l of engine oil and add 4 l of petrol. Army gear oil 8 E is good to −40°.
- **p.27, warm-up:** "im Sommer 5, im Winter 15 Minuten laufen, bis das Kühlwasser 50°, das Wechsel-Getriebe handwarm und der Öldruck auf 3 atü gestiegen ist."
- **p.55 / p.76, gun:** "Mündungskappe ab, bei Eis auch die durchschießbare." / "…im Winter Mündungskappe auf." / "Im Winter tarne diesen Fleck [muzzle-blast patch], denn er wird schwarz."
- **Status of the "frozen road wheels" story.** Wheels freezing in overnight is attested (TF p.35, 43; HAM [S]). The popular variant, that the Soviets timed dawn attacks to catch Tigers frozen in place, is **[F]**; I found no source for it.

### 2.4 Damage and field fixes
- **p.5:** "Er ist von außen nicht kaputt zu kriegen — aber von innen … Die Gefahr sitzt in der Wanne!"
- **p.13:** "2 Stunden braucht der Tigerfahrer, um den Wagen in Schwung zu bringen. Sonst bleibt er wegen einer Kleinigkeit liegen."
- **p.16, fire:** "Wenn es brennt, leuchtet die Warnlampe neben der Fahrersehklappe, spritzt die Anlage Löschflüssigkeit (wenn sie es nicht tut, muß der Ladeschütze auf den Knopf am Feuerlöscher drücken) und Du mußt sofort Kraftstoff und Motor abstellen, nicht Vollgas geben … Sonst werden die Löschgase abgesaugt … Sie ist für 5×7 Sekunden Löschen gebaut." / "Feuerlöscher gegen einen neuen tauschen, wenn der Druck unter 4 Atmosphären sinkt." / "Brandursache ist immer Kraftstoff oder Öl in der Wanne."
- **p.26:** oil pressure of at least 3 at when idling, 7 at when driving; if it falls, "sofort den Motor abstellen. Sonst frißt sich der Motor fest."
- **p.31, limp mode:** "Hat der Motor zu wenig Öldruck, verhindert ein Kurzschließer höhere Tourenzahlen. — Du sollst Deinen kranken Tiger nur noch in die Werkstatt fahren."
- **p.34, final drives:** "Seitenvorgelege: Dichtungen nachsehen. Wenn Öl ausgeworfen wird, müssen sie bald erneuert werden. Sonst muß ein neues Vorgelege rein."
- **p.38:** "Jage sie niemals über 3000 U/min …" / "Beim Marsch Kanone auf 6 Uhr drehen und zurren." / Driving through walls: "Durch den Lüfter wird der ganze Schutt angesaugt, der Kühler wird eingedeckt … Der Motor wird heiß und fällt aus."
- **p.41, emergency gear change (*Notschalten*):** quoted in the table. **p.44, recovery:** quoted in §1. The driver "baut die Vorgelegewellen aus, setzt aber die Schrauben wieder ein, damit das Wechselgetriebe ausgeschaltet ist, die Bremsen aber wirken."
- **p.55, when not to fire:** with the muzzle brake loose or shot off (it takes 70% of the recoil), with the recoil brake losing oil (25%), with the recuperator losing air (5%), or with the split pin missing from the opening lever.
- **p.56, misfire table:** the recoil brake holds 5.1 l; recuperator 55 at; fuses 15 A and 40 A, with spares from the driver; emergency battery switch; "Wenn die Ölsicherung ausgeschaltet hat, darf nicht geschossen werden."
- **p.61, "MG-Barometer":** diagnosis by where the bolt stopped, giving the immediate remedy and the root cause (dirt, sloppiness, fatigue, or breakage, which means a new part or the armourer).
- **p.62, powered traverse drill:** "Mo-Fa-Fu-La-Ba" (Motorluke, Fahrerluke and Funkerluke closed, lamps off, path clear). Then the loader selects the traverse range, the radio operator sets the selector lever to "Turm", the loader lifts the emergency lever, and the gunner uses the rocker pedal. **p.63:** fault table, quoted in §1.
- **p.79:** boresighting and the *Notvisier*; "Beim Marsch immer Waffen zurren. Trotzdem wandern sie durch die Erschütterung aus."
- **p.10, supplements (not in the scan; worth obtaining):**

  - No. 12: "Die selbsttätige Löschanlage"

## 3. Firing trials against Tigers (Soviet and Allied)

**NIIBT Kubinka, 25–30 Apr 1943.** Target: Tiger #121 (Fgst. 250004, s.Pz.Abt. 502). Report approved 18 May 1943.
- **What was fired** [P via S: TA, WS, ZEN]:
  - 13 guns, 5 anti-tank rifles (PTRS, RES 20 mm, Blum 15P-2 and 43P, Ostrovsky), the KB-30 grenade, the TMD-B anti-track mine, a bounding mine from Factory 627, and 37 mm cannon on LaGG-3 and Il-2 aircraft.
  - The 107 mm, the 122 mm howitzer and the 152 mm scored no valid hits.
  - **The published summaries give no results for the rifles, grenade or mines (gap).**
- **T-70 45 mm, subcalibre:** penetrated the upper side at 200 m (20 mm hole) and the 62 mm lower side at 350 m. At 500 m it left an 80 mm-deep dent with bulging on the inside [P via S: ZEN, WS].
- **45 mm M-42:** standard AP failed against the side even at 100 m (30 mm dent). Subcalibre penetrated the lower side at 500 m, and the upper side and turret side at 350 m. "Cracks and spalls began to appear in the armour" [P via S: WS].
- **ZiS-2 57 mm:** penetrated the side at 1,000 m. Against the front at 500 m: no penetration, dents 20–65 mm deep, **but the weld seams failed**.
- **6-pdr:** penetrated the side at 1,000 m and the turret side at 800 m. **Churchill 57 mm:** side at 625 m. **75 mm M3:** side at 400 m (M61) and 625 m (M72).
- **F-34 76 mm (the T-34's gun):** no penetration even at 200 m, with AP, subcalibre or HEAT.
- **76 mm AA gun:** 1 of 3 hits on the turret side at 500 m penetrated at the top edge, and **the turret-to-roof weld broke over 1,680 mm**.
- **85 mm 52-K:** penetrated the side at 1,450 m and the lower front at 1,000 m. At 1,500 m it split the plate along earlier damage; the upper front took only a 30 mm dent at 1,500 m.
- **122 mm A-19 at 1,500 m:**
  - Round 1 went through a breach in the lower front plate and **out through the hull rear**.
  - Round 2 hit the turret; the mantlet had been removed before the test. TA says it struck the turret front, tearing out a 58×23 cm chunk. WS says it struck the turret rear, through the opening left by the mantlet, tearing out 500×230 mm. Either way it **ripped the turret off its ring and moved it 540 mm**.
  - Round 3 ricocheted off the roof, leaving **through-cracks**.
- **Conclusion** [P via S: WS]: the armour tended to brittle failure, and the welds were weak.
- **Soviet directives, 1943** [P via S: ZEN]:
  - Crews were to **destroy knocked-out Tigers with demolition charges or incendiary (KS) bottles**, because the German recovery units were so effective.
  - An anti-tank-rifle crew on the Voronezh Front knocked out a Tiger in July 1943 by letting it pass and firing into the rear from about 20 m (ЦАМО ф.6231 оп.26929с д.2 л.26).

**Soviet field trial, 16 Jun 1944** (2nd Bn, 63rd Guards Tank Brigade, against a captured Tiger) [P via S: TA].
- ZiS-5 76 mm subcalibre at 300 m: "Shot #1 … hit the **turret ring and jammed it**. Shot #2 hit the **gun mantlet and destroyed the gun**."
- 85 mm at 600 m against the front: "The **driver's observation block was knocked through**. The rounds exploded inside the tank and it caught fire."
- Report conclusions: most vulnerable were "the suspension and the armour behind it, side armour, and driver's observation device". Partial penetrations still knocked out the crew with spall, and "mechanisms and sights are destroyed".

**Allied trial in Tunisia, Jul–Aug 1943** (hull of a knocked-out Tiger on transport tracks) [P via S: WS]. 75 mm HE hits cracked the side plate. A 6-pdr hit at the joint between the front plate and the roof opened a 6-inch crack. There was spalling on the inner faces, plate quality varied, and two plates shattered.

**British trial, Shoeburyness, 15–22 Mar 1945** (Tiger Fgst. 250570, built Oct 1943, ex-s.SS-Pz.Abt. 101; later Tiger) [P via S: TA, WS].
- 17-pdr APDS through the mantlet destroyed the recoil cylinder.
- A 6-pdr ricochet off the mantlet broke through the hull roof and damaged the gearbox.
- HE bursting under the pannier floor tore the floor out, split welds and badly damaged the tracks.
- 25-pdr splinters holed a radiator; the tank would overheat "within five minutes".
- 20 mm AP from aircraft went through the engine deck, wrecked a radiator and pierced a fuel tank, which did not ignite.
- A missing road wheel exposed the lower side; an AP hit there set a fuel tank on fire.

## 4. Crew accounts: damaged but still fighting

## 5. Early-Tiger specifics

1. **Hatches.**
   - As originally designed there were 4 crew hatches: the commander's drum-cupola lid (15 mm), a **loader's roof hatch** (350×500 mm), and the driver's and radio operator's hull hatches (560 mm, each with a periscope) [S: T1].
   - A **turret escape hatch** (circular, right rear, replacing the right pistol port) was added in **December 1942** [S: T1, TE, HAM/J&D].
   - Of 503's original 20 Tigers, **7–9 had the escape hatch** and the rest had two pistol ports [S: T1].
   - Zabel's report confirms a loader's hatch in Feb 1943 [P via S].
2. **Spare track links, 1942–43.**
   - A turret-side stowage scheme was decided in May 1942 but not delivered until **about April 1943** (5 left, 3 then 2 right; combat links only) [S: T1/J&D; HAM].
   - Before that, crews improvised. 3./503 carried them **on the driver's front plate, clamped with a bar** (Zabel, Feb 1943) [P via S]; the lower front plate was also common [S: TE].
   - A 10-link box in the turret was nominal and not on early vehicles [S: TE].
3. **Manual traverse.**
   - The gunner had a **horizontal handwheel in front of him**, with a lockable grip [S: T1]; the elevation handwheel was on his right, about 1° per turn [S: T1].
   - **720 handwheel turns per 360° is [F]**: an unsourced forum post (AHF t=54727). The same post gives 25–60 s for powered traverse, matching HAM.
   - **No loader's auxiliary traverse handwheel is documented for the Tiger I** in TF pp.62–63 or in the T1 turret surveys. The "680 turns by the loader" figure online refers to the **Tiger II** [F]. D 656/22 would settle it.
   - TF p.63 confirms hand traverse was hard work. If the 720 figure is right, a full turn by hand would take several minutes (inf.).
4. **Did hydraulic traverse need the engine?** **Yes.**
   - TF p.62: the driver starts the engine; the radio operator sets the selector lever to "Turm"; the loader engages the range, "**links — langsam, rechts — rasch**", and lifts the emergency lever; the gunner uses the rocker pedal; "**Fahrer: Gas geben, wenn's ganz schnell gehen soll**."
   - The drive comes off a power take-off on the Olvar gearbox, which also drives the bilge pump [S: HAM]. I infer the selector chooses between turret and bilge pump.
   - Speeds: HAM gives **25–60 s per 360°** "depending on engine speed" [S]. WP says "about a minute" [S].
   - WP, citing Carius p.23, gives more detail. Early production had only a low range, capped at 6°/s regardless of rpm. A selectable high range came later: 19°/s at 2,000 rpm and over 36°/s at 3,000 rpm, with a minimum of 0.1°/s. **Treat as unverified.** The 19 s-at-2,000-rpm figure matches Tiger II data. TF p.62 (Aug 1943) already shows both ranges and speed rising with rpm. When the fast range reached the Tiger I is unresolved.
   - British trials found the high-speed traverse hurt accuracy; the best hit rates came from low-speed laying plus the handwheel [S: TE].

## 6. The 12 most game-relevant facts

1. **A Tiger could be put out of action without a single penetration.** In 6 h it took 227 anti-tank-rifle, 14 45/57 mm and 11 76.2 mm hits: vision blocked, MGs destroyed, gun stuck in recoil, radio dead, turret briefly jammed, engine fire, two torsion bars broken, fuel tank leaking. It still drove 60 km. Sources: TF p.4 [P]; Zabel via Schäfer and the AHF/TE translation [P via S].
2. **Crew-station consumables fail first.** The 5 cupola blocks and 2 turret-side blocks were designed to be swapped from inside, with 4 spares in the turret [S: T1/J&D]. A hit can still jam the holder, as on Zabel's cupola [P via S]. The driver's visor jams "im Winter und bei Beschuß"; the crew fix is on TF p.40 [P]. Hits on the smoke dischargers set them off inside the tank and blinded the crew, and they were deleted in June 1943 [P via S; S: T1/J&D].
3. **The gun has hard interlocks.** Do not fire with the muzzle brake loose or shot off (it takes 70% of the recoil), with the recoil brake losing oil (25%, 5.1 l) or with the recuperator losing air (5%). The *Ölsicherung* cuts the firing circuit. Crews can top up oil and re-charge the recuperator to 55 at (TF pp.55–56) [P]. A mantlet hit can shear the mount outright (Zabel [P via S]; Soviet 1944 trial [P via S: TA]).
4. **The turret ring is the soft spot.** Fragments in the 10 mm gap jam the turret [S: T1/J&D]. Single hits jammed turrets on Tiger 131 [S] and in the Soviet 1944 trial [P via S]. Turret bolts were doubled from vehicle 201 after turrets were torn loose [S: J&D §3.4.2.13]. A 122 mm hit shifted a turret 540 mm [P via S: WS].
5. **Powered traverse needs the engine running and three crewmen.** Speed rises with engine rpm (TF p.62) [P]. An open engine hatch stops the turret at 4 or 8 o'clock; the fix is to crank by hand while pulling on the gun with a rope (TF p.63) [P]. The hatch opens only with the turret at least 64° to the right [S: T1].
6. **Towing.** Tiger-towing-Tiger was avoided ("vermeide es", TF p.44 [P]) or forbidden except in emergencies, because of transmission and engine overload [S: Jentz via TM]. It was still done with crossed ropes [P via S: POW report]. Standard recovery was 2–3 Famos in tandem, with the final-drive shafts removed so the brakes still worked [P: TF p.44]; the 502 report says 3–4, and 5 uphill [M/P].
7. **Interleaved running gear is costly to repair.** Changing an inner wheel means taking up to 13 wheels off and jacking a whole side with 2 × 30 t jacks (TF p.36) [P]. A broken torsion bar is a workshop job (TF p.35) [P], but a Tiger with broken arms still drove 60 km [P via S].
8. **Track work.** The fitting procedure uses the tow cable wrapped 3× round the sprocket and needs the engine running; target sag is 4 fingers; each link weighs 30 kg (TF p.35 [P]; HAM [S]). There was no factory spare-link stowage before April 1943. Links hung on the front plate were shot away by the first hit [P via S]. Tigers were lost to air attack *while* changing tracks [S].
9. **Winter drill.** Park on brushwood, chip out the ice, and move every 2 h; thaw wheels with a blowtorch; clamp the clutch pedal down; a flat battery freezes at −10°; start with the inertia starter; pump hot coolant from one Tiger into another; warm up for 15 min (TF pp.17, 22, 27, 35, 43) [P].
10. **Fire and fuel.** The automatic extinguisher gives 5 × 7 s and a warning lamp at the driver's visor; the loader has a manual button; do *not* rev, or the fans suck the gas away. "Brandursache ist immer Kraftstoff oder Öl in der Wanne"; 1 l of fuel in the tub can blow the engine hatch off (TF pp.14–16) [P]. Zabel's engine fire came from a shot-off exhaust cover and was put out automatically [P via S].
11. **Cooling is fragile.** Stop at 95 °C and check four things (TF p.19) [P]. Rubble sucked into the fans or tarps over the grilles cause overheating (TF pp.38–39) [P]. Splinters in a radiator meant overheating in 5 minutes (British trial 1945, later Tiger) [P via S].
12. **Built-in limp modes.** Low oil pressure makes the governor cap rpm, and the tank is to be driven "only to the workshop" (TF p.31). There is an emergency gear change with a key (TF p.41), emergency steering with the brake levers [S: HAM], a backup battery for gun firing (TF pp.56–57; T1), and the left sight telescope as a fixed 1,000 m emergency sight (TF p.79) [P].
