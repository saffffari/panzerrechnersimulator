# Authentic tank-audio reference shortlist

Research date: 2026-08-11. This catalog deliberately separates recordings that may be licensed for
the game from listen-only references. A public video or game recording is **not** permission to copy
its soundtrack. Preserve the invoice, license text, original download, and source URL for every
purchased or downloaded library.

## Priority acquisition

### 1. Pole Position Production — Tiger II

- Product: <https://sonniss.com/sound-effects/tiger-ii/>
- Provenance diary: <https://warthunder.com/en/news/4020--en>
- Preview: <https://soundcloud.com/polepositionproduction/tiger-ii-tank-audio-preview-demo-montage>
- Authenticity: **A**. The only operable Tiger II at Musee des Blindes, Saumur, recorded by Pole
  Position. Its Maybach HL 230 P30 is the closest available source to the late Tiger I's HL 230 P45.
- Contents: 678 files, 24.9 GB, 96 kHz/24-bit; 16 onboard perspectives and 12 exterior perspectives,
  plus engine, exhaust, tracks, gearshifts, turret, hatches, levers, starts, stops, idles and driving.
- License: commercial royalty-free single-user library after purchase; archive the exact license.
- Recommendation: **buy this first**. It can supply nearly the entire Tiger movement/mechanism bed.

### 2. BOOM Library / Pole Position — World War 2 Tanks

- Product: <https://www.asoundeffect.com/sound-library/world-war-2-tanks/>
- Full metadata: <https://www.boomlibrary.com/metadata/00_WW2_Tanks_Metadata.pdf>
- Authenticity: **A/B**. Real Panther, Hetzer, T-34, Sherman, B1 bis, Chaffee, Somua and Valentine.
- Contents: 395 files, about 27 GB, 96 kHz/24-bit. Four synchronized onboard perspectives per
  vehicle, exterior passes, engine/exhaust/tracks/interior, handling and cannon shots.
- Best use: Panther is a second HL 230-family reference; Hetzer supplies cramped German-AFV cabin
  texture; T-34 supplies the enemy vehicle. This is broader than the Tiger II set but less specific.

### 3. Sound Ideas / Sounddogs — Military Sound FX

- Product: <https://portal.sound-ideas.com/Product/1152/Military-Sound-FX>
- Authenticity: **B**. Real WWII German Panzer plus Sherman, Stuart, Bulldog, Duster and modern LAVs.
- Contents: 729 royalty-free sounds, 33.3 GB, 24/48; onboard and exterior drives, crew compartment,
  rear track, exhaust, tread squeaks, engine pings and bodywork clanks.
- Best use: secondary variation and non-engine mechanical texture. Less precise provenance than the
  Tiger II and BOOM libraries, so do not use it as the sole Tiger identity.

## Free or low-cost useful references

### Authentic vehicle and crew behavior

- Tiger 131 start procedure and genuine start sound, Tank Museum:
  <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6-eGqAJlcg> (engine start at 07:42). **Listen-only.** Tiger 131
  currently uses an HL 230 rather than its captured HL 210, so label it restored-running authenticity.
- Tiger 131 overview, Tank Museum: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fhj9Mzp0-eA>. **Listen-only.**
- M1A1 crew-intercom gunnery recording:
  <https://freesound.org/people/Baelphazoar/sounds/568003/>. Authentic 2005 Grafenwoehr intercom;
  CC BY 4.0. Main/coax reports are muted, but commands, hot-mic tone, engine/hydraulic whine and the
  ejected aft-cap hitting the deflector are valuable interior references.
- CC0/public-domain tank engine loop:
  <https://freesound.org/people/qubodup/sounds/200303/>. Provenance is a US-government video, but the
  vehicle is nonspecific. Useful only as a placeholder or layer, not Tiger identity.
- Zapsplat tank category: <https://www.zapsplat.com/sound-effect-category/tank/>. Museum/collector
  recordings with interior and exterior perspectives; license varies by account/download. Treat each
  file independently and retain its record.

### Modern analogues for perspective and acoustics

- Pole Position Strv 121 / Leopard 2A4:
  <https://pole.se/product/strv-121-leopard-2a4-1989/>. Twenty onboard perspectives, nine exterior,
  four-channel interior Ambisonics and interior impulse responses. Modern diesel, therefore not an
  engine substitute, but excellent for hatch/buttoned filtering, compartment resonance and camera
  perspective design.
- Challenger 2 interior live fire:
  <https://ukdefencejournal.org.uk/first-hand-look-inside-a-challenger-2-tank-as-the-gun-is-fired/>.
  **Listen-only.** Useful for the breech-dominant interior report and mechanical aftermath.

  <https://www.dfmgsignaturesfx.com/m1a1-main-battle-tank>. Royalty-free buyout; 70+ sounds including
  inside/outside driving, 120 mm firing, coax, .50 cal, turret, hatches and mechanisms. Designed rather
  than a documented multichannel source library, so use below Pole/BOOM in the trust hierarchy.

## Incoming fire and armor impacts

No verified, clean, licensable recording was found of an occupied heavy tank being struck by WWII AP
or HE from an interior microphone. This is expected: a representative AP penetration is destructive,
dangerous, and unlikely to leave a usable recorder. Do not label marketplace sounds `authentic AP/HE`
unless the vendor supplies the projectile, plate, microphone position and test provenance.

### Closest physical references

- AAAV/EFV Mk 44 trials filmed from inside BMP-1/M60 targets:
  <https://www.facenews.ua/video/2018/309540/>. Genuine ballistic-test imagery of 30 mm AP effects and
  spall inside targets. **Reference only; source audio/license is not cleared.**
- Swedish Strv 103 live-fire trials: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rqq1XtHauoc>. Genuine test
  footage covering projectile impacts and behind-armor effects. **Listen-only.**
- Soviet pressure measurements summarized from AP and HE impacts:
  <https://www.tankarchives.com/2017/03/under-pressure-part-2.html>. Not audio, but valuable for keeping
  the pressure/hearing response physically ordered by caliber, distance, impact and penetration.
- The Recordist steel-plate bullet session:
  <https://therecordist.com/slow-motion-bullets/>. A real firearm/steel recording account; the plate
  was piercing while thinner metal produced a duller whack. Investigate the associated licensed
  library before acquiring.
- Sound Ideas Guns library metadata lists recorded `bullet impact on heavy metal` variations:
  <https://portal.sound-ideas.com/Files/SpecificationFile/Guns_Sound_Effects_Library.pdf>. Potential
  licensable small-arms source; verify that the purchased take is a ballistic recording, not Foley.
- Pole Position Vehicle Stone Chip Hits:
  <https://sonniss.com/vendors/poleposprod/>. Not gunfire, but five inside/outside body, rim and wheel-
  well perspectives are useful for quiet gravel/rifle-rain layers once combined with real ballistic
  transients.

## Recommended implementation vocabulary

Never make `impact` one sample. Select and mix independent layers from the struck plate/location:

1. **Contact transient** — extremely short steel crack/thud, harder and brighter for AP, broader for HE.
2. **Hull mode** — low-mid structural ring whose pitch/decay depends on plate size, bracing and hit point.
3. **Shock/pressure** — sub/low-frequency impulse and momentary compressor hit; stronger for HE and
   penetration, not a generic exterior explosion pasted into the compartment.
4. **Interior sympathetic rattle** — racks, tools, optics, spent cases, hatch hardware and suspension.
5. **Debris/spall** — granular steel fragments only when the armor response warrants it.
6. **Exterior event leakage** — filtered blast/crack through open hatches and ports; strongly attenuated
   when buttoned.
7. **Human hearing response** — short overload, tinnitus and recovery, scaled separately from loudness.

Perspective states should be authored from shared sources rather than unrelated samples:

- **Interior/buttoned:** engine block-borne energy, transmission, tracks through hull, rattles and crew;
  little direct exhaust or muzzle report.
- **Open hatch/unbuttoned:** crossfade exterior air path into the same synchronized event, with wind and
  weather masking; never merely turn up the buttoned mix.
- **Outside/on foot:** exhaust and track radiation dominate by angle and distance; gear changes and
  suspension impacts remain synchronized with the onboard stems.

For small-arms strikes, avoid the Hollywood ricochet whistle on every hit. A rifle burst on thick armor
should read as irregular, very short hard ticks/thuds plus local plate/rack resonance; true ricochet tails
should be occasional and angle-dependent. HE should be a pressure-and-structure event, while AP should
be a sharper contact event whose post-contact debris changes radically between stop, partial damage and
penetration.
