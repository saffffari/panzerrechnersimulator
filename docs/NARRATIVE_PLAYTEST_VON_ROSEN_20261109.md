# Narrative playtest from Richard von Rosen's memoir: outline (9 November 2026)

Status: **outline for the owner's approval; nothing is built.** Source: Richard Freiherr von
Rosen, *Panzer Ace* (Greenhill 2018; German original 2013), written at about 90 from his
wartime diary and letters home. **The book's text was not available here.** Everything below
comes from published summaries, one excerpt and battalion histories, gathered on 9 November
2026; each fact carries its mark: **C** two sources or an authoritative one, **S** a single
source, **GK** general knowledge not verified. Events are adapted, never his prose.

Correction to what I told the owner from memory: he was a **gunner in a Panzer III in 1941**,
not a Tiger gunner in the winter of 1943; by January 1943 he commanded a Tiger. No source
reached supports a wound on Kursk's first day; an excerpt has him in command on 7 to 10 July.

## Episodes

Each: what the sources say, what we would adapt, what we would exaggerate for play.

### 1. The Manych, January 1943: the long road and the broken tank

- **Sources (S, battalion level, not attributed to him):** Tigers guarding river crossings
  on a front full of gaps; road marches of 65 to 107 km with a halt every 20 km; crews sitting
  30 hours with broken-down Tigers; a Tiger through stream ice hauled out by six 18-ton
  tractors and two Tigers; Tigers towing Tigers; about 35 % of the tanks fit. In the sister
  companies at Vessely (9 January) one Tiger took 227 anti-tank-rifle, 14 57 mm and 11 76 mm
  hits and drove 60 km back.
- **Play:** a march with maintenance halts; a breakdown; a night alone on the steppe with the
  crew (cold, watches, a probe by infantry); recovery at dawn under tow; a rearguard at a
  crossing against T-34s; the drive back in a tank that should not still run.
- **Adapted:** that these things happen to his tank. His own first action was not found.
- **Exaggerated:** the number of attackers at the crossing; the hits compressed into one day.
- **Runs on what exists:** winter map, repairs and faults, camp and half-track, infantry,
  T-34s, 45 mm guns. **The recommended first build**, though the least well sourced for him.

### 2. Miasoedovo, 7 to 10 July 1943: the wooded valley

- **Sources (S, a memoir excerpt; the best documented):** he leads four Tigers, holds a
  reverse slope in the heat with rotating watches, then takes one Tiger (with Feldwebel
  Weigel) to scout a wooded valley. He halts about 400 m out, takes three hits from two
  hidden assault guns, none through; fires back; reverses out while two Tigers cover. The
  worry is the final drive. The Soviet tanks withdraw the next morning. A school classmate
  is killed on the battle's first day; mail comes forward; a lull means washing.
- **Play:** overwatch with two AI Tigers (platoon mode exists), the creep forward, reading
  where the fire comes from, the decision to back out, inspecting the damage, the night.
- **Adapted:** the valley's layout; which rounds strike where.
- **Exaggerated:** little. Its strength is that almost nothing "happens" and it is tense.
- **Needs:** a summer map and assault-gun opponents of 1943.

### 3. Goodwood, 18 July 1944: digging out

- **Sources (C, with conflicts in the numbers):** heavy bombers from about 05:45; he shelters
  in a dugout under Tiger 311 and blacks out; a Tiger is flipped, another vanishes in a direct
  hit; about 15 dead; the maintenance section wiped out; radios dead. Crews dig out tanks
  buried to the turret, clear trees, refit tracks. By about 10:00 six or eight run; the gun
  sights are out of alignment and about one round in three hits. Two more are lost, to
  mistaken German 88 fire or to overheating (the sources disagree).
- **Play:** almost no shooting for the first half: live through it, find the crew, dig, refit
  a track, boresight a sight knocked out of true (the game already has that fault and its
  repair), then fight with a gun that misses.
- **Adapted:** the order of the work. **Exaggerated:** nothing needed.
- **Needs:** a Normandy map and the bombardment. The strongest anti-war material of the four.

### 4. Hungary, late 1944: the fire brigade (later)

- **Sources (S):** he commands 12 Tiger IIs with a flak company, sent from gap to gap; bogged
  and broken Tiger IIs towed again and again. **Needs** the Tiger II; not for now.

## How the story is told

Written material only, as the project's first principle allows: orders on the printer strip,
the radio net, the map, and **diary pages and letters home written by us** in his situation
(his own were the memoir's basis; we do not have or reproduce them). One page before and
after each episode. No narration.

## What must not be glossed over

- A reviewer doubts his claim not to have known of the war of annihilation; he writes with
  pride and few qualms (S). The game is an anti-war work and does not take his view as its own.
- "Panzer Ace" is the English publisher's title; no personal tally was found.
- **GK, to verify before use:** the 4th Panzer Division's part in crimes of 1941; 503's Tiger
  IIs in Budapest in October 1944 (the coup that installed the Arrow Cross). His personal part
  in either: not found.
- Kill and loss figures in the battalion account are German claims.
