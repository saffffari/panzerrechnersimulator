# Environment reference board — 5 September 2026

These are contemporary photographs with archive or museum provenance. The observations below
come from inspecting the photographs; the proposed game treatments are interpretations. A dated
aftermath photograph establishes that photographed condition, not every earlier day of combat.
Images remain at their original hosts and are not added to the game's redistributable asset pack.

Current scope: Act I is the Stalingrad prototype; Act II is Oderbruch/Seelow; the executable's
Act III still uses Halbe. `STATUS.md` and `LINUX_4090_HANDOFF.md` make Berlin and the Reichstag the
canonical destination. Halbe material is retained as regional/reference material, not a replacement
for that destination. Modern satellite imagery helps terrain and geography; it does not establish
1940s vegetation, buildings, damage, or weather.

## Winter city — Stalingrad

### S1 — Vorovsky Street, February 1943

![Vorovsky Street after the fighting](https://stalingrad1942-1943.ru/sites/default/files/styles/foto/public/photos/%D0%9A%D0%9F__19287_10280%20%D0%9D%D0%95%D0%93__4728_2.jpg?itok=SYtHQ6Kd)

[Museum record](https://stalingrad1942-1943.ru/node/2971): Stalingrad Battle Museum-Reserve,
KP 19287/10280, negative 4728/2; photographer unidentified. Visually inspected in the browser.

Visible: a nearly continuous damaged street frontage, empty window openings, broken rooflines,
debris concentrated against the buildings, a leaning pole with support struts, a worn light road
surface, and a small bare tree silhouette beyond the right building.

Worldbuilding treatment: give streets edges and former property structure. Use sparse bare
deciduous trees and damaged trunks where streets/gardens warrant them; do not turn the open
approaches into a forest. Vary snow and exposed travel surfaces instead of a clean white blanket.
The photo does not establish an exact species or a regional tree density.

![Stalingrad center after liberation](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c0/RIAN_archive_602161_Center_of_Stalingrad_after_liberation.jpg)

## April countryside and village edges — Oderbruch / Seelow

### O1 — Oder crossing, April–May 1945

![Oder crossing photographed by Valery Faminsky](https://www.seelowerhoehen.de/aktuell/images/5_ODer000.jpg)

### O2 — Casualty transport near Seelow, April 1945

![Roadside trees and damaged property near Seelow](https://www.seelowerhoehen.de/aktuell/images/5_Verwundetentransport000.jpg)

[Museum captions and provenance for O1 and O2](https://www.seelowerhoehen.de/ausstellung-detail.php?id=5):
Valery Faminsky; Arthur Bondar's Private Collection. Both photographs visually inspected.

Visible: a low, open river horizon with a thin line of sparse/bare trees; timber crossing members;
large roadside trunks; mostly open branch silhouettes; small budding/leafing shrubs; damaged
fences; brick and branch debris at property margins; a clearly traveled dirt road.

Worldbuilding treatment: preserve broad fields and water/drainage corridors. Concentrate young
grass, reeds and litter at margins rather than covering every surface equally. Mix bare and
emerging deciduous growth; April does not justify uniform summer foliage. Keep the road readable
between cluttered edges. The monochrome photographs do not determine exact grass colors.

## Forest approaches — Halbe regional reference

### H1 — Forsthaus Halbe after the fighting

![Forsthaus Halbe after the fighting](https://image.jimcdn.com/app/cms/image/transf/dimension%3D323x1024%3Aformat%3Djpg/path/sc7e524e8acffa9cf/image/ia6e2c7f4f266ffab/version/1527708765/forsthaus-halbe-ausbruch-stra%C3%9Fenk%C3%A4mpfe.jpg)

[Provenance and place](https://www.geschichte-erinnern.de/das-jahr-1945/kessel-von-halbe/):
Red Army photograph, Archiv Uwe Klar, reproduced by Gerd-Ulrich Herrmann. Visually inspected;
the available preview is only 323 × 228, so fine architectural details remain unresolved.

Visible: a lodge beside tall straight trunks; vehicles squeezed into the approach; a foreground
filled with cut/fallen timber, including piles and crossing pieces.

Worldbuilding treatment: dense woods should form boundaries and restricted routes. Place logs,
twigs, needle/leaf litter and damaged timber in meaningful groups rather than uniformly scattered
props. This aftermath arrangement must not be silently moved into an earlier battle phase.

## Canonical destination — Berlin / Reichstag

### B1 — Reichstag approach, May 1945

![Reichstag approach and trees in May 1945](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/assets/bundesarchiv/de/_processed_/e/d/csm_BArch_Bild_183-J0422-600-007_Berlin_Reichstag_Schlacht_Flak_da5c519cd0.jpg)

[Bundesarchiv record and caption](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/das-letzte-kriegsjahr-1944-45/):
BArch Bild 183-J0422-600-007, cropped reproduction, photographer unidentified. Visually inspected.

Visible: surviving trees with sparse foliage, shattered trunks, a damaged gun emplacement,
cartridge cases, timber, masonry, and a continuous field of small debris below the ruined dome.

Worldbuilding treatment: layer large landmark geometry, medium obstacles and dense small debris.
Retain photographed surviving trees. Do not use the subsequently cleared Tiergarten of winter
1945–46 as proof of the tree cover during the April battle. [Berlin's monument authority dates
that later clearing](https://www.berlin.de/landesdenkmalamt/denkmale/highlight-denkmale-der-alliierten/grossbritannien/mitte/englischer-garten-647982.php).

## Placement and sound rules for this pass

1. Keep structural edges legible: roads, walls, fences, ditches, property boundaries and forest
   margins determine where material collects and where tanks can travel.
2. Use the Pizza bundle's woody geometry, stones and groundcover as the visual vocabulary.
   Seasonal derivatives retain the original wood and remove actual leaf geometry; they are not
   generated replacements for the vendor art.
3. Grass uses a bounded near field with real stalk geometry, coherent wind and tread response.
   Exact pixel width varies with camera distance; avoid thick crossed cards and distant shimmer.
4. Match sound placement to physical objects and processes: wind at openings/canopies, branch or
   rubble contact when disturbed, water beside the actual channel, and shelter behind geometry.
   The photographs themselves provide no evidence of specific sounds; these are authored physical
   interpretations unless separately supported by testimony.
5. Preserve the distinction between source-matched landmarks, documented regional conditions and
   authored connective scenery. This board guides the ecological/detail pass; it does not certify
   that every currently generated scene is a historical reconstruction.
